# Studio Brief

*Front-door session output, 2026-08-02/03 — the machine-facing Phase-0 input, distilled from the concept doc beside it. This is the shape Maya consumes.*

## What is this story about?

A product manager and his four AI sidekicks share a break-room headquarters. An alarm — PROBLEM! over a falling checkout graph — detonates the room: each sidekick instantly builds a brilliant, magnificent, completely wrong solution, and Sean is in the chaos with them, egging them on. A hollow ship, and the alarm re-blares. Sean steps back — the film's one quiet beat — asks the faceless USER in the doorway a single question, and learns the truth on a screen-replay: the customer simply couldn't find the buy button. The same team then executes the tiny right fix as a five-second F1 pit stop. The graph rockets; the grey USER blooms green. The emotional engine is autobiographical: judgement is not being above the chaos — it's the practiced act of stepping back out of it.

## Who is this character?

**Sean** — the Full Stack PM, center frame. Pencil-test human (existing `sean-anchor` Bible). Cycle 1: in the chaos, thinking he's conducting. The pivot: swivels his desk chair away from the monitors, eyes closed, deep breath. Speaks exactly once — the question to the user. The narrator is also Sean: his real recorded voice, serene throughout.

**Claude** — small terracotta plush robot (existing `claude-mascot` Bible). Earnest, over-caveating, gentle voice. Wrong-build: a 40-page sticky-flagged strategy document, still growing.

**Codex** — cloud creature with a terminal-symbol face. Terse build-monotone. Wrong-build: rebuilds the entire checkout stack, flawless, unasked. NEW — needs Cy Bible.

**Gemini** — blue-purple star creature. Bubbly option-overload. Wrong-build: fifty redesign concepts taped over every surface. NEW — needs Cy Bible.

**Grok** — grey gremlin, bat ears, sharp grin. Feral edgelord. Wrong-build: sledgehammers the wall, builds a guerrilla-marketing rocket. NEW — needs Cy Bible.

**The USER** — faceless grey pencil-test humanoid, t-shirt reading "USER," small in the doorway. Colorizes green — still faceless — at the moment Sean understands the real problem. Never a specific man or woman. NEW — needs Cy Bible.

## What is the tone?

Pencil-test warmth wearing Tom & Jerry / Looney Tunes slapstick; the Goofy "How To" shorts ("Two Weeks Vacation," "Father's Weekend") supply the audio grammar — serene narrator, music, SFX, a little dialogue — and the gag grammar: narrator sets up, picture punches; signs deliver punchlines; repetition-with-variation. Sincere under the silliness: one protected quiet beat at the step-back. Voiced model-cliché personalities as an extra comedy layer over fully-visual storytelling.

### What this is NOT

- Not a résumé with cartoons — no credential lists, no title cards of skills; the story IS the claim.
- The mascots are never dumb — every wrong-build is brilliant, only misaimed. Never imply AI is useless.
- The user is never the joke — the complaint is always legitimate; faceless ≠ pathetic.
- The step-back beat is never undercut — no gag, no VO irony, for its full held duration.
- The narrator never acknowledges the chaos — his composure is the straight man; break it and the engine dies.
- Not sanded down for portfolio politeness — the silliness is the self-portrait.
- Not vector-clean, not cel-shaded, not anime, not 3D-rendered — pencil-test register rules apply (`pencil-test-colored`).
- No real AI-company logos, wordmarks, or brand assets — the mascots are original characters built from public model clichés.

## What is the format?

~75–90 seconds, hard ceiling 2:00. Three movements: Quiet Morning (~15s) → Chaos Build (~35s) → Step Back + Pit Stop (~30s). 16:9 landscape. Two-cycle structure: alarm → wrong-ship → re-alarm → right-ship.

## What is the target medium?

The portfolio About-Me page: 16:9 web master (MP4/WebM), muted-autoplay assumed, captions baked in. Vertical cut-downs deferred until the master exists.

## What is the deadline?

None — ships with the audited About page. Draft-before-pro budget posture; stub passes first.

## What are the non-negotiables?

- The narrator's composure never breaks; no VO line ever describes a visible joke.
- The narrator/picture gap IS cycle 1; the gap closes at the step-back and stays closed through the pit stop.
- The step-back is held 2–3 seconds past comfort: eyes closed, swivel away from monitors, no gag.
- The Sean character speaks exactly once — the question to the user.
- Every beat reads on mute: TV text, signs, and labels carry the plot; captions baked in.
- The four wrong-builds are named and escalate one per beat: Claude's 40-page document, Codex's full rebuild, Gemini's fifty concepts, Grok's wall-demolition rocket.
- The alarm fires twice (second louder: "PROBLEM (STILL!)"); the SHIP IT bell rings twice (hollow, then earned).
- The user's answer is shown, not told: the screen-replay of the cursor circling, never finding the buy button.
- The pit stop runs ≤5 seconds, F1-choreographed, one micro-task per mascot.
- The USER is grey until the moment of understanding, then green — always faceless.
- Squash-and-stretch and smear frames on takes; move → dead-stop hold → burst on the alarm and pit stop.
- Character continuity per the five turnaround refs; hero-frame anchor `art-viz/route-c--gpt-ref.png`, backup `art-viz/route-c--nb2.png`.
- The film ends on one narrator button line over the colorized user's smile.
- Mute-parity applies to payoff VARIATION, not just plot: the bell's two rings read differently in picture (sag + confetti misfire vs. full-swing + clean cannon) and alarm-2 is visually bigger than alarm-1 (frame shake / red flood).
- The step-back and the question have both landed by second 60. Movement 3 may grow to ~35–40s inside the 2:00 ceiling; the held beat and the replay's legibility are never the compression victims.
- Build order: the first production stub is a 2-second pit-stop slice (≥2 characters, one micro-task each). No full boarding before it passes.
- No full production spend before a blind-judged 30-second scratch narration passes; the narrator fallback path is named in writing.
- Sean's question and the narrator's button line are written and cold-read with one working PM before boards.
