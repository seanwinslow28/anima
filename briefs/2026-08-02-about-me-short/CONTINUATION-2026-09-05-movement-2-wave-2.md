## NEW LAWS EARNED THIS WAVE — all paid for

### Motion

1. **A FACE IS A PER-FRAME CONSTANT, PER CHARACTER.** The 3½ v1 locked every position and
   fixture and no faces; the constant slapstick energy went into jaw-drop screams. v2 wrote one
   face constant per character and they held. Write them every time, in the "the same … in every
   frame" form.
2. **TAKE VERBS BUY TAKES.** *snaps, jerks, whip, freeze, staring* → shock takes. The register
   and vocabulary blocks are constant and always full-energy; **the words you give it to spend
   that energy on decide the acting.** *"A quick clean turn and then stillness"* is a small action
   at full snap — the calm law, working.
3. **THE CLOSED-MOUTH CONSTANT DOES NOT HOLD THROUGH A BREATH OR A SCAN — two phrasings failed
   (v2, v3).** Per the S07 blink lesson, stop buying rolls against a phrasing. The lever that
   remains is the timing law: **delete the event** (drop the breath). Sean's own v4 is what landed.
4. **A DESCRIBED SUBJECT IS WASTED WORDS** (prompt-how-much): the frame carries identity. v3
   cut the hair/stubble/eyes paragraph; keep the face *constant*, drop the face *description*.
5. The standing ones, unchanged: register + vocabulary blocks verbatim; fill the seven seconds,
   a loop is the ideal shape; jumps allowed with speed verbs and a landing constant; zero
   negation; locks as constants not rest states; never pass character refs to Seedance;
   deleting an event moves a beat, shortening a sentence does not; no absolute time cues; smears
   come from repeated or rotational action.

### Stills

6. **The description line is an identity LOCK, and it overrides the reference's mood.** The
   turnaround's ¾-front Grok is drawn on the snide side; every friendly Grok on screen came from
   *"a wide friendly cartoon grin"* correcting it. Sean has since redrawn the sheet — but the
   principle stands for every character: **describe the mood you want; the reference gives the
   design.** Reference-only made identity worse.
7. **Five references in one call conflate the same-colour-family pair** (Codex/Gemini) on
   gpt_image_2 as on NB2. Split them, or close in the web app.
8. **A matte from a difference mask fails on thin extremities and on figures over dark
   fixtures.** `post/merge_edits.py` stays for what it is good at (a lone figure on open floor);
   do not trust it blind.
9. The standing ones: rest pose; ¾ front; the scale law (TALL anchor + ceiling); a FRAMING
   clause deletes what it excludes; the look lives in post; normalise every frame; preserve
   superseded work in `old/`.

### The Grok wording hazard stands: *"a round grey cartoon creature with big pointed ears and a wide friendly cartoon grin."* Verbatim. Never paraphrase.

```
motion/gen_image.sh       <prompt> <ratio> <out.json>              # from scratch
motion/gen_image_ref.sh   <prompt> <ref> <ratio> <out.json>        # one design ref (edits)
motion/gen_image_refs.sh  <prompt> <out.json> <ref...>             # N role-split refs (plates)
motion/composite.sh       <prompt> <char> <scene> <out.json>       # one character into a plate
motion/generate.sh        <prompt> <start> <out.json>              # motion, one keyframe
motion/generate2kf.sh     <prompt> <start> <end> <out.json>        # motion, two keyframes
```

Job JSON is a **one-element list**: `j[0]["status"]`, `j[0]["result_url"]`,
`j[0]["params"]["prompt"]` (read it back to confirm the prompt landed). Contact sheet:
`ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf "fps=12/7,scale=480:-1,tile=4x3" -frames:v 1 sheet.png`.
