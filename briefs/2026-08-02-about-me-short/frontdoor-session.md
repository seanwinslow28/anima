### expand:voice-format   <!-- workshop depth -->
- observations: Sean is torn between silent-film-with-music and fully voiced characters, leaning voiced: "it would be funny to take the cliche's of each model and give a voice and personality based on those cliche's." The tension: voiced maximizes personality comedy; the piece lives on an About page where autoplay is typically MUTED, and Tom & Jerry slapstick (his stated tone) is natively silent.
- options:
  1. **Fully voiced, cliché-forward**: each mascot gets a voice built from its model's public reputation (Claude: earnest, over-caveating, gentle; Codex: terse monotone build-speak; Gemini: bubbly overload of options; Grok: chaotic edgelord). Tradeoff: maximum personality showcase and quotable lines, but a muted first-play loses half the piece, and it adds script + casting/TTS + mix lift.
  2. **Pure silent + music** (Tom & Jerry native): personalities carried entirely by pantomime, timing, and animation acting. Tradeoff: bulletproof on mute, cheapest, and the strongest proof of animation craft — but the model-cliché comedy Sean explicitly wants gets much harder to land.
  3. **Gibberish-voice hybrid** (minions/R2-D2 lens): nobody speaks words — each mascot gets a signature SOUND palette (Claude warm hums, Codex keyboard-clack chirps, Gemini sparkly arpeggios, Grok feral cackles) + expressive pantomime; all information lives in picture and TV-screen text. Tradeoff: keeps mute-safety AND a voice-personality layer; loses quotable cliché dialogue.
  4. **Voiced with silent-proof staging**: full cliché voices, but staged so every beat ALSO reads silent (visual acting first, dialogue as garnish) + bold captions on the TV. Tradeoff: best of both, but it's a discipline the whole production must hold, not a checkbox — every gag storyboarded to work twice.
- recommendation: **lean 4** — Sean's instinct (voiced clichés) is the right comedy engine for a personality-showcase piece, and the mute risk is real but designable-around: write and board every beat to read silent first, then let the voices double the joke for viewers who click in. - open_questions: whose voice is Sean's? (self-record vs. TTS vs. silent protagonist among voiced mascots — a Tom-and-Jerry move where the HUMAN is the quiet one could itself be the judgement joke); caption treatment for mute-play.
