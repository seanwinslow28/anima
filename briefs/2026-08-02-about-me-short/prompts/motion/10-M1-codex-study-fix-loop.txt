1950s theatrical cartoon slapstick, hand-drawn colored-pencil pencil-test animation on cream paper, held on twos with visible line boil. Fixed camera, locked tripod, one continuous shot.

The lavender cloud creature leans in close to the black server rack, head tipping side to side as it studies the tangle of cables. It springs straight up the face of the rack, grabs the hanging cables in both stubby arms and whips them into a tight coil in a blur, then drops back down to the floor. It lands square, leans in and studies the coil again, tips its head the other way, and springs straight back up to yank the cables tighter.

Its face is exactly one pale chevron and one short straight bar, the same chevron and the same bar in every frame. It lands on both feet on the floor after every leap. It stays small, no taller than a quarter of the black server rack beside it, in every frame.

Squash and stretch on every accent, smear frames through the fast arcs, snappy timing, elastic overshoot, comic energy.
