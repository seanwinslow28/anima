## LOCKED DECISIONS   <!-- orchestrator-owned; APPEND-ONLY -->

- [L1] SPARK (verbatim): "I have an idea for a short or maybe a mini series on Youtube. It's in the animation style of a 90's cartoon. Similar art styles to Ren and Stimpy (Nickelodeon) and Cow and Chicken (Cartoon Network). It's about a kid who wants to go viral and tests out all of the various Youtube viral channels that people do. AI trends, Cooking videos, Workout Videos, What I eat in a day, everything we can think of. It's very much admiring while also mocking these people. Admiration for their abilities to want to help the world and share their knowledge, but also mocking some of the cliche's and lameness of some that are doing it just to make money and the value they provide is subpar with lots of editing. That would be the kids arc."
- [L2] premise: A — "The Algorithm Made Me Do It." The engine is addiction/obsession with the algorithm's dopamine loop, not ego-driven fame-seeking. Each genre-of-the-week is a fix; the kid physically warps deeper into it (body horror as comedy).
- [L3] style-tone route: Route 1 — Ren & Stimpy gross-out. Wet linework, extreme close-ups, unhinged proportions, body horror as comedy.
- [L4] risk resolution — target of the joke: the joke is on the kid and his own awful attempts, never on real/specific creators. The clichés of each genre are parodied generically (cooking, workout, what-I-eat-in-a-day, AI trends, etc.) and then the show builds its own original bad-attempt videos from them — no callouts, no real-person parody.
- [L5] the ending: the thing that finally goes viral is NOT any engineered genre attempt — it's an accidental, unproduced, humiliating moment (a real breakdown/getting caught off guard) that outperforms everything he manufactured. He does not learn authenticity as a warm lesson; he treats it as data and immediately starts trying to engineer "being real" as the next genre to game. No growth, no moral, the treadmill continues (mini-series-friendly: next episode can open with him already ruining "authenticity").
- [L6] the stakes/clock: tied to his first day at a new school (or the elementary/middle → high school transition) — he wants to walk in already defined as "that AI guy," "that cooking guy," "that workout guy," etc., reinventing his social identity before anyone there has a prior read on him. The algorithm itself is personified as an in-universe presence/motif that visibly reacts to and pressures him, escalating the body horror episode to episode.
- [L7] single objective: both a genuine attempt at a real, published YouTube series (leaning primary) AND a deliberate anima pipeline test — a new art style (90s Nicktoon/Ren & Stimpy) and a new story outside the Pencil Test lineage. Project context = anima's second reference implementation, not just a portfolio piece.
- [L8] scope/format: Phase 0 plans a single validated PILOT episode (~60-90s, one genre attempt) — not the full season. If the pilot's register + economics prove out, the remaining season (new-school-deadline arc, one-genre-per-episode) gets brainstormed as a follow-on, not authored now.
- [L9] pilot genre: "AI guru" — the kid fakes AI mastery (voice filters, face filters, deepfake overlays, reacting to/showcasing real AI tools). Sean specifically wants this episode to double as a sandbox for playing with different real AI generation tools/styles inside the kid's in-universe attempted content — a meta layer, not just a plot choice.
- [L10] the algorithm-presence's form: a cute, friendly AI-assistant avatar/orb — the "helpful chatbot mascot" the kid props up as a cohost in his fake-guru videos. It reads adorable and helpful whenever the kid is watching/recording, but has brief, unobserved moments of demonic/deranged behavior the instant the kid looks away from his phone/computer — the surface is a mask; the audience sees the tell, the kid doesn't (yet).
- [L11] creative reference — Bo Burnham's "Welcome to the Internet" (INSIDE): the carnival-barker/ringmaster performance mode — seductive, showman, "come one come all" cadence — plus its signature tonal whiplash (mundane and horrifying content delivered in the exact same chipper register, escalating list-style). Fold this into how the algorithm-avatar PITCHES content ideas to the kid: a ringmaster sales-pitch energy for dangling trend options, with the tonal-whiplash technique available as a device (e.g. genuinely useful AI content and unhinged/dangerous suggestions delivered with identical enthusiasm).
- [L12] protagonist: named Aiden (a nice unforced echo of "AI" for the AI-guru premise), incoming freshman starting the first day of 9th grade/high school — his big chance to leave grade school behind and pick a new persona. Design-bearing visual contrast: huge, unblinking, camera-ready eyes + too-wide showman's grin the instant he's on-camera/recording, vs. droopy, tired, twitchy real eyes + a nervous stammer the instant the camera's off. The performed confidence is a mask; the tell is instant.
- [L13] target audience: adults / young adults (Gen-Z through older-millennial), Adult Swim-adjacent — the generation that grew up on 90s Cartoon Network/Nickelodeon and now lives the algorithmic burnout the show is about. Not a children's show wearing a kid-cartoon skin; humor/gross-out/darkness plays at full Ren & Stimpy + Bo Burnham strength, not softened for a younger rating.
- [L16] art-viz route (Step 2.5, run live 2026-07-03 — this brief was originally synthesized 2026-07-02, before ART-VIZ existed as a chain stage; run now to close the gap): **Route A — Ren & Stimpy faithful** locked as ai-guru-pilot's chosen rendering register — Sean confirmed the orchestrator's recommended lean, which matches the register already locked at micro-expand ([L3]). ### micro-expand (workshop continuation — risks 2 & 3)
- observations: Sean confirmed risk 1 is resolved (joke is on the kid, no real-creator callouts) and asked to workshop risk 2 (ending resolving too neatly/saccharine) and risk 3 (does "wants to go viral" need harder stakes) before locking further.
- options — risk 2 (the ending):
  1. Cyclical/nihilistic: his "biggest hit" is an accidental humiliating clip he didn't intend — success IS the horror, not failure. Nobody learns anything (classic Ren & Stimpy amorality).
  2. Escalation/treadmill: no lesson learned — he just moves to chasing the next platform/algorithm, implying it never ends.
  3. Permanent mutation: his body-horror transformations never reverse; he becomes a background "content creature" in future episodes — visual gut-punch, cruelty without malice.
  4. Bait-and-switch irony: an accidental, unproduced, raw moment (a meltdown, getting caught off guard) is what actually goes viral — thesis delivered as a further joke on him (he's mortified it worked), not a warm growth moment.
- options — risk 3 (the stakes/clock):
  1. Self-imposed subscriber-count deadline tied to a real-world event (birthday, first day of school, a bet) — serializes cleanly per episode, supports a visible subscriber-counter gag.
  2. A rival kid (already semi-famous, or competing for the same "big break") — peer pressure, not an adult antagonist.
  3. The algorithm personified as an in-universe entity/motif (an ominous notification chime, a creature, a presence) that rewards/punishes him directly — ties the clock straight to the addiction premise.
  4. Grounded real-world stakes — money/status needed for something concrete in his actual life.
- recommendation: combine risk-2 options 1+4+2 into one landing — his "biggest hit" is an accidental, humiliating, unproduced moment (option 4's irony) that becomes the horror-success (option 1's amorality), and rather than learning anything he immediately starts trying to engineer "authenticity" as the next genre to game (option 2's treadmill) — dark, funny, series-friendly, never saccharine. For risk 3, combine options 1+3: a self-imposed subscriber deadline tied to a real-world event (structural clock, mini-series-friendly) plus personifying the algorithm itself as an in-universe presence (thematic engine that escalates the body horror).
