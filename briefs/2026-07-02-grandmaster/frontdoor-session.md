### micro-expand
- observations: The spark already carries one fragile high-value intuition worth protecting through every later stage: the grandmother reveal has to land as earned lineage, not a convenient magic-backstory shortcut, and the finale's reversal (a child publicly disarming an adult) sits right on the edge of triumphant vs. mean. Both are named as risk options below.
- options:
  - **3 alternate premises** (different emotional core / genre collision than the obvious sincere-tribute reading):
    1. Ghost-mentor buddy comedy — grandma's spirit literally haunts/coaches him, bickering, visible. Genre collision: grief-as-precision-violence (John Wick register) told as ghost buddy comedy. Tradeoff: very funny, but a talking ghost turns her into a prop and fights the "carried by object/room/photo, never dialogue" restraint that makes the tribute land.
    2. Piñata-as-legendary-monster — the neighborhood has a "hall of fallen challengers" (other kids' failed attempts shown in Act 1), the piñata itself built up as an undefeated boss across the year, staged with shonen-boss-fight beats. Tradeoff: adds scale and spectacle, but shifts stakes from human (redemption in front of peers) to game-like spectacle and risks crowding out grandma's throughline.
    3. Rival kid, two-hander — a second humiliated kid trains alongside him; the return is a shared triumph or friendly duel. Genre collision: samurai film x buddy underdog-sports movie. Tradeoff: relationship texture and a world that could extend past one short, at the cost of the tight solo-tribute structure.
  - **3 style-tone routes** (visually/registrally distinct):
    1. Deadpan minimalist — static symmetrical frames, driest possible timing, the samurai sequence played dead flat with sudden bursts of violence-as-slapstick (Napoleon-Dynamite-adjacent restraint).
    2. Full Tartakovsky homage — sincere, operatic, silhouette-and-hold, "timing is a song" (the spark's own implied register — named here as a chosen route, not assumed by default).
    3. Unreliable mockumentary — shot as talking-head interviews with party guests about "the incident," cutting to a reenactment that escalates in exaggeration each retelling. Tradeoff: very now, very funny, but the ironic documentary distance fights the sincerity the tribute needs.
  - **3 risk questions:**
    1. Does "she was secretly a warrior" read as earned lineage, or as a device that lets the kid skip the actual work of training?
    2. Does the finale's reversal — a child disarming and humiliating an adult at his own kid's party — read as a fair comeback or as cruelty? Does the dad need a beat that keeps him sympathetic/comedic rather than punished?
    3. Does the triumph resolve too cleanly — does the piece let one perfect return erase a year of being laughed at, with no scar tissue acknowledged?
- recommendation: Keep the sincere-tribute premise as the spine (none of the three alternates replace it), but consider folding a sliver of alternate #2 in as texture only — a one-shot "hall of fallen challengers" beat in Act 1 that raises the piñata's legend without turning it into a boss fight. Style: lean route 2 (full Tartakovsky homage) — it's what "samurai-cinema sequence" and "candy erupts like stylized blood" are already asking for. Of the three risks, #2 (is the dad's humiliation mean) is the sharpest live edge — worth resolving explicitly before we lock anything else.
- open_questions: none yet — surfaced to Sean at the gate below.

### expand:host-dad-reversal
- observations: Sean picked this axis at the deepen gate — a genuinely live, unresolved tension: the finale needs the disarm to land as a real Tartakovsky-grade threat (stylized violence homage), but the target of that threat is an adult at his own kid's party, which risks the piece's own rule ("comedy from collision, never from mocking") applying asymmetrically — protected for the kid, unguarded for the dad.
- options:
  1. **Awed bystander (structural-mechanic lens)** — the "horror" beat becomes astonishment/disbelief, not fear of retribution; he was never framed as vindictive, just the ritual's operator, baffled by what he's witnessing. Tradeoff: softens him toward sympathetic from the start, but drains the power-reversal charge the ending is currently built to deliver.
  2. **The piñata was always the target (emotional-core lens)** — restage the disarm so its target reads unmistakably as the string/piñata, not the dad; he laughs, delighted and rattled, no punitive charge at all. Tradeoff: fully removes the risk, but Act 1 stages HIM as the one who rigs the string to make the kid fail — cutting the reversal on him specifically leaves that Act-1 beat without a payoff.
  3. **Convert, not victim (sports-movie failure-mode/analogy lens)** — one beat of visible respect after the disarm: a startled grin, the first adult to actually clap. Tradeoff: safest, most crowd-pleasing, fully protects the dad — but risks sanding off the finale's dark comic edge; a samurai disarm with a real weapon should carry a little genuine menace or the homage rings toothless.
  4. **The ritual is what breaks, not the man (tonal-register lens)** — his horror is aimed at the situation, not himself: "the piñata bit just got permanently weird," not "I've been bested." Keeps the Genndy "hold-then-reaction" comic DNA (the same technique already locked into the style bible) while routing the joke's target off his dignity and onto the absurdity of what just happened.
- recommendation: Fuse 4 + a sliver of 3 — land the beat as "the ritual just got weird" (his horror is at the situation, not personal defeat), but let one half-beat of real respect flicker through first: his hand freezes on the cut string, he looks at the kid, astonishment and respect show for a beat before he defaults to bewildered horror at the ninja star itself. This keeps the dark, genuinely threatening edge the Tartakovsky homage needs (a real weapon, a real disarm) while extending the piece's own no-mockery rule to the dad symmetrically with the kid — protecting the intuition rather than picking a side (soften him completely, or let the joke land on him) that would flatten it.
- open_questions: none — this is a staging/reaction-shot note, buildable inside the existing pencil-test/Genndy register; no new style vocabulary needed.
