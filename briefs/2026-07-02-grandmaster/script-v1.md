# FIRST LICKS — Script v1 (screenplay-form pass of beat-sheet v2)

*Formalized 2026-07-24 from the Sean-ratified [`beat-sheet-v2.md`](beat-sheet-v2.md)
(DR #33 a–p). Human-authored route: structure per `script-writing`, voice kept lean —
the film is near-wordless; every dialogue line below is a LOCKED line from the record.
Page ≈ minute; ~4¼ pages at natural pacing (the stopwatch gate rules the final cut).
Beat numbers in the margins key every scene back to the sheet.*

---

FADE IN:

**EXT. NEW JERSEY SUBURBS — DAWN** *(v2 #1)*

Quiet streets. Clapboard colonials, telephone wires, cracked
sidewalks — drifting RIGHT in one slow cinematic pan, like the
opening of a legend.

Faint, distant: CHILDREN SCREAMING. Playing. It grows.

One house stands out — balloons on the mailbox, streamers on
the fence.

**EXT. BIRTHDAY BACKYARD — CONTINUOUS** *(v2 #2)*

Letterboxed. Dawn light. Hanging from the great tree branch
like a sacred relic: a PIÑATA — Sparkle Horse, gaudy, bootleg,
magnificent.

SLOW PUSH-IN. One streamer TICKS against the fence. The only
sound — under the screams, still rising.

Hold. Past comfort.

Optional: a reverent low-angle of Sparkle Horse's face. Dead
straight.

SMASH CUT — THROUGH A WALL OF VHS STATIC:

**CAMCORDER POV — THE PARTY — DAY** *(4:3 inside the widescreen; timestamp; audio clipping)* *(v2 #3–9)*

Mid-"HAPPY BIRTHDAY" chaos.

The lens inventories the party by performance: BRITTANY (9)
princess-mugs — smile ON, smile OFF. THE DAD narrates his own
greatness over footage that undercuts him — behind him the
bounce house sags. THE MOM, wordless, rotates the cake to its
good side for the lens.

On tape, unnoticed: a GRILLER flips dogs with tongs — scratches —
pauses — sniffs — resumes. Double-take at the lens. A wide-eyed
"shit" face.

Kids swarm Brittany showing off for the camera. And far behind
all of it, small, at the edge of frame — someone.

The ZOOM MOTOR whines. RACK FOCUS. The party audio thins.

THE KID (8). Too-big square glasses. Shoulders in. The only
person not performing. The camcorder accidentally does its
first real hold.

>                     CAMERA-HOLDER (O.S.)
>          Wh--... When did Linda get the sad
>          clown statue?

The kid looks at the lens — STARTLE — the camera YANKS away —

CLICK.

One full second of BLACK. Dead silence.

**EXT. PARTY YARD — DAY** *(the film's own camera — composed, held, locked; it never leaves him)*

The kid, alone at the fence line. World ambience fades up.

**THE PARTY TEXTURE** *(v2 #10)*

The dad works a fence-rail row of koozies like ammunition —
swaps, cracks, CRUSHES the empty on his bicep.

**THE CATCH** *(v2 #11)*

Kids playing catch, apart from him. A WILD OVERTHROW rockets
at the kid's head —

His hand SNAPS up and catches it. He wasn't looking. He was
looking at his shoes.

He holds it up. Offering.

A kid walks over, takes it — zero eye contact — jogs back.
Nobody says anything. Nobody saw anything.

**THE STREAMER** *(v2 #12)*

A strand of RED crepe streamer tears loose, drifts across the
grass, settles at his feet.

He stares. Picks it up. Runs it through his fingers —
mesmerized, somewhere else.

He pockets it.

**THE RITUAL** *(v2 #13)*

The dad, center stage, stick in hand:

>                     THE DAD
>          Societal rules say everyone gets a
>          turn and a trophy nowadays...
>              (scanning; finding him)
>          ...so let's give this little freak
>          back there first licks. Get it over
>          with.

The kid doesn't hear it. He's with the streamer.

The stick is already airborne — CLONK. Off his head.

>                     THE DAD (CONT'D)
>          Heads up.

The kids LAUGH. The trance is broken.

**THE EPIC-DEFLATE** *(v2 #14)*

The kid picks up the stick. Something flares behind the
glasses — he'll show them. He's going to be the hero of this
party.

And the film believes him: WIDE SHOT. Long hold. Slow pan.
Low angle — a tiny warrior squaring up to a hanging god.
FRONT-FACING: he SPRINTS at camera, determination burning.
He LEAPS —

CUT TO: WIDE SHOT — SIDE VIEW. Flat. Normal speed. One
continuous unglamorous shot:

He jumps. Swings. The dad YANKS the string — the piñata hops —
the kid FACE-PLANTS.

The kids ROAR. The adults wince, chuckle awkwardly. The dad
laughs from the gut — the only adult enjoying it.

**THE EXIT** *(v2 #15)*

The kid picks himself up out of the grass. Walks out alone
through the laughter. Brittany's satisfied little smile as he
passes.

>                     THE DAD (O.S.)
>              (chuckling)
>          Alright, who's next? How 'bout you,
>          twinkle toes.

**INT. THE KID'S HOUSE — HALLWAY — LATE AFTERNOON** *(v2 #16 — THE NO-JOKE ZONE BEGINS)*

The front door closes softly. He walks the hallway on
autopilot — the walk he's done after every bad day of his
life. Toward her door.

**INT. GRANDMA'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

He opens it. Stops.

Dark. Still. A fan of a life on the walls: kung-fu movie
posters. A shelf of hand-labeled VHS tapes. Knick-knacks.
Polaroids-through-the-years — her and him, every year of him.

Her chair. Empty.

The reflex has nowhere to land. He stands in the doorway of
the absence.

**THE TAPE** *(v2 #17)*

The TV. The VCR. One tape sits half-out, like always. Movie
night was the comfort. She's gone. The ritual isn't.

He presses PLAY.

4:3. Tracking lines. A HOME MOVIE: the two of them in the
backyard — patty-cake blocks, fence-walking, catch. Her
boombox on the grass, playing HER SONG.

And on the degraded mic — HER LAUGH.

The only voice she will ever get. He almost smiles.

**THE GLITCH** *(v2 #18)*

The image TEARS — tracking error — and underneath, taped over:

1970s FOOTAGE. A young woman drilling martial forms. Small.
Fast. Serious.

The same moves.

MATCH CUT: his childhood game — her warrior form. Game — form.
Patty-cake — block. Toss — catch.

The kid stares. The inference detonates behind his eyes. Not
on screen. In him.

**THE BOX** *(v2 #19)*

On the tape: the young woman finishes, kneels, and places a
red headband INTO A WOODEN BOX.

His eyes drift off the TV. A small double-take — eyes only.

There. Half-hidden. The closet floor. The same box. Older.

He pulls it out.

**INSIDE** *(v2 #20–22)*

The worn RED HEADBAND — a small phoenix stitched at the edge.

A 1970s PHOTOGRAPH: her, young — and small, small like him —
in a tournament gi, face marked up, hair wrecked... and her
hand raised in victory. A ref mid-count. A much bigger
opponent looking at the floor.

He turns the photo over. Her handwriting, decades old, written
for no one but herself:

>          *"Fall seven times. Rise eight."*

Beneath the photo: a lift-out tray. Rows of NINJA STARS,
neat as a sewing kit.

His mouth forms a silent *woah*. A glance at the door —
parents? — and he pockets ONE.

**IGNITION** *(v2 #23)*

He ties on the headband. Too big. It slips.

He ties it again, tighter. It holds.

HER SONG RISES — full arrangement —

**MONTAGE — THE YEAR** *(v2 #24–35 · one song · the boombox is the calendar)*

**END OF SUMMER** — boombox NEW, tree full green:
— First strike: cocked, off-balance, dead serious. The sack
  barely moves. *(#24)*
— The neighbor's mower kicks up rocks — his hand SNAPS one
  from the air. The neighbor, over the fence: confused. *(#25)*
— From the pool, spaced apart: the cooler-lid chop · every
  grocery bag in one trip · the horse-stance beside a bored
  donkey · squaring up to a flailing tube-man · crane stance
  on the mailbox at dawn. *(#26)*

**THE TREE — FIXED FRAME No. 1:** the locked replica
composition. Green canopy. The sack hangs re-tied. He is tiny
beneath it. *(#33 — D1)*

**FALL** — boombox DUCT-TAPED, tree rusting; the grey hoodie,
glasses still on:
— One falling rust leaf. One clean swing. Two halves drift. *(#27)*
— RAIN. The downspout dumps a hard column onto him — he holds
  horse-stance beneath it, eyes shut, enduring. A parent's
  silhouette in the lit doorway. Doesn't come out. *(#28)*
— The paper-route run, boombox under one arm. *(#29)*

**THE TREE — FIXED FRAME No. 2:** same frame. Rust leaves.
The sack's rope frayed. He stands steadier.

**WINTER** — boombox SNOW-CAPPED, tree bare ink; the puffy
coat — and the glasses are GONE:
— Dead-still meditation in falling snow, breath fogging. *(#30)*
— From the pool, spaced: three snowmen felled in sequence —
  the last carrot drops · the icicle kata · the snowsuit
  pins his arms — a rigid marshmallow, dead serious · the
  snowman AMBUSH — the neighbor, invested now, recoils. *(#31)*

**THE TREE — FIXED FRAME No. 3:** same frame. Bare black
branches, snow. The rope re-tied clean. He bows to it.

**SPRING** — boombox SUN-BLEACHED:
— Bottle-cap throws — tighter, faster — until: a falling
  blossom petal PINNED to the fence. Across the yard, the
  neighbor gives one imperceptible nod, shuts off the hose,
  and is never seen again. *(#32)*
— The string-yard: taut lines, hanging cans, one pass —
  silence — then the delayed clatter-symphony. *(#34)*

**THE TREE — FIXED FRAME No. 4 — THE REPLICA** *(v2 #35)*

Same frame. FULL BLOSSOM. The boy stands small and still at
the base, mastered, headband fitting.

Beside him: her space. Held empty.

The wind rises and fills it with petals. One beat.

THE SONG ENDS.

**EXT. THE KID'S MAILBOX — DAY** *(v2 #36)*

A bootleg-gaudy envelope. His name on it.

Inside, in kid handwriting: an invitation — and a HAND-DRAWN
DOODLE: a stick figure face-down under a tree, piñata swinging
above, HA HA HA.

He looks at it a long moment. Puts it in his pocket.

**EXT. PARTY YARD — NEXT SUMMER — DAY** *(v2 #37)*

Same yard. Same ritual. Everything escalated in identical
framings — the pony corner is a petting zoo now; the bounce
house is LOCKED, kids pressed against the netting like
inmates.

The kid stands at the gate. The headband fits.

**THE MIRROR** *(v2 #38)*

The dad clocks him. Grins. Tosses the stick at him — the
exact toss —

SNATCHED out of the air. He wasn't looking. He's staring at
the dad.

The grin flickers.

**THE STRIKE** *(v2 #39–41)*

The kid POSES — the film goes with him this time: wide, held,
low. He charges. LEAPS, both hands on the stick —

The dad grabs for the string — the yank, the signature —

MID-AIR: the kid's free hand draws the STAR from his pocket
and THROWS —

It SEVERS the string at the dad's grip — the dad recoils,
lets go — the star flies on and LODGES in the fence. The knot
holds. The piñata stays.

The STICK comes down and SPLITS Sparkle Horse's belly.

WHITE FLASH — over the sound of a small body landing clean.

**THE MONEY SHOT** *(v2 #42)*

TIGHT: his face bottom-right, eyes off-camera, extreme
post-strike samurai pose, stick in frame. Over his shoulder:
the piñata, intact.

Hold.

The belly YAWNS OPEN — and a RAINBOW GEYSER floods down,
liquid candy-guts pouring out of the gut. The head stays
intact, goofy as ever.

He does not move a muscle.

**THE SILENCE** *(v2 #43)*

EXTREME WIDE: the whole party ringed in awed silhouettes. The
dad, shocked. The kid, still in the pose.

Long. Held. Silent.

A drenched kid licks his own cheek. The verdict registers —

The mob DETONATES — every kid SCREAMING, charging the geyser —
Brittany FIRST, elbows out, still awful.

**THE WALK** *(v2 #44–46)*

The kid rises out of the pose. Spins the stick once. Walks —
through the chaos, no look back.

The dad stands apart. Stares at the ninja star buried in his
fence. One involuntary flash of — respect?

He shakes it off.

>                     THE DAD
>              (muttering)
>          Yea... whatever.

At the gate: a LOLLIPOP bounces out of the melee, end over
end — SNATCHED from the air without a glance. Unwrapped.
Popped in.

First and last licks.

He walks home — toward the house where she lived — the stick
resting on his shoulder. An echo of HER SONG rises, far away
and getting closer.

**EXT. SUBURBAN ROAD — DUSK — THE FINAL IMAGE** *(v2 #47)*

WIDE: his small silhouette down the long road home.

The camera PULLS BACK — slow — through the dark yard — until
the foreground fills with the gutted SPARKLE HORSE, hanging
from its branch, belly split open, head goofy and intact.

The sacred object, de-throned. The kid, mythic, small,
walking away behind it.

Hold.

FADE TO BLACK.

*(Optional credits stinger, mockup-gated — v2 #48: a camcorder
cassette, hand-labeled "BRITTANY'S 9th — DO NOT TAPE OVER."
A timestamp from next year's party plays over it anyway.)*

**END**
