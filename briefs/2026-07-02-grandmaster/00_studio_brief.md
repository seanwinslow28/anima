# Studio Brief

*Front-door live session, 2026-07-02 — the machine-facing Phase-0 input, distilled from the concept doc beside it (concept.md). This is the shape Maya consumes.*

## What is this story about?

A small, timid kid is humiliated at a backyard birthday party when he can't hit the piñata. Alone in his room — surrounded by the kung-fu movies his late grandmother left him, and the discovery that she was secretly a warrior — he ties on her old headband and trains for a year, entirely on his own initiative. He returns to the same party and destroys the piñata in a full samurai-cinema sequence, severing the host dad's string mid-air with a real ninja star. The piñata erupts candy like stylized blood. The wimpy kid is wimpy no more. It's a tribute to a lost grandmother, wrapped in a genre-collision comedy.

## Who is this character?

**The Kid** — the smallest kid at the party, timid, easy to laugh at, with a specific silhouette-readable tell (shoulders in, avoids eye contact, glasses a size too big) that visibly resolves by the return. Wears his late grandmother's faded headband — the load-bearing continuity object, too big at the start, fitting by Act 3.

**The Late Grandmother** — never on-screen as a living character. Present only through the room (kung-fu-movie posters, an old TV), one framed photo with the kid, the faded headband, one unfinished VHS tape, and a single faded snapshot of her young and mid-flying-kick — the reveal that she was secretly a warrior. One ghost-beat maximum: her reflection in the dark TV glass. She never speaks; the training is entirely the kid's own choice, never her instruction.

**The Host Dad** — runs the piñata ritual: calls the kid up, tosses the stick, works the string. The unwitting fulcrum of the reversal, not a villain — his reaction at the disarm reads as "the ritual itself just got weird," with one half-beat of real respect first. Never mocked, never diminished.

## What is the tone?

Sincere, with a dark comic edge — the emotional gravity and staging of a samurai film welded onto a suburban backyard birthday party. Comedy is engineered to come only from that collision. A homage to Genndy Tartakovsky (Samurai Jack): timing-as-a-song, silhouette-and-hold, stylized non-naturalistic "blood." ~2–3 minutes, three acts.

### What this is NOT

- Not glossy, not anime-clean, not 3D-rendered, not motion-graphics-clean.
- Not saccharine — no eulogy, no speech about the loss, no funeral, no dialogue from the grandmother, ever.
- Not a "tough grandma" stereotype — she's a specific person, carried by artifacts, not a cutout.
- Not mean toward the kid — the collision is the joke, never him.
- Not mean toward the host dad either — his reversal reads as the ritual breaking, not as a man humiliated for laughs.
- Not a magic-backstory shortcut — the grandmother's reveal is evidence (a photograph), not instruction; the kid chooses and does the training himself.
- Not a clean, consequence-free ending — the Act-1 bratty kid returns just as oblivious in Act 3; the world doesn't notice or change, only the protagonist did.

## What is the format?

A ~2–3 minute short, three acts (Humiliation → Turn + year-long training montage → Return + the strike). Per-shot letterbox is a tool for peak-tension beats only, not the base aspect ratio.

## What is the target medium?

Portfolio centerpiece + festival short as the 16:9 master, plus a vertical social cut-down built around the candy-geyser strike as a standalone hook. Web-delivered masters (MP4/WebM) plus the social crop.

## What are the non-negotiables?

- Timing is a song: hold pre-strike frames 2–4 seconds past comfort; move → dead-stop hold → burst.
- Raw Primal-style visible ink linework: sketchy, heavier-weight hand-drawn line, gritty painterly texture, warm earthy desaturated palette — figure and background share the same raw hand-drawn treatment. *(Supersedes the earlier "no black outlines / flat silhouette" draft of this rule — the ART-VIZ pass, run 2026-07-03, locked Route B (Primal grit) over Route A (Samurai Jack's flat-silhouette register); the two are mutually exclusive Tartakovsky registers, not two readings of one rule.)* This names a register (`primal-sketch-grit`) outside the current six-register vocabulary — the nearest existing register, `line-art-only`, mandates bold *uniform* outlines, the opposite of Primal's raw, weight-varying sketch. Cy's Bible pass must either secure a register extension or get an explicit human ratification of the closest achievable approximation before GENERATE. Do not silently soften this rule.
- The candy burst is staged as a stylized oil-geyser: over-saturated, non-naturalistic, arterial-scale, cut on the moment of impact — the piñata is this piece's "robot."
- The headband: too-big-swallowing-his-head at the start, fitting by the return; visible in every training beat and the return.
- The kid stays recognizably himself in every frame — same design, same proportions, same headband.
- The grandmother is carried only by object, room, tape, and one photograph — never by dialogue. One ghost-beat maximum (her reflection in the dark TV glass). She never speaks and never instructs; the training is entirely the kid's own choice.
- The host dad's reversal reads as "the ritual broke," not "the man lost" — one half-beat of genuine respect before his bewildered reaction; never mocked, never punished for sport.
- The Act-1 "I'm totally getting one of these for my party" kid reappears obliviously in the Act-3 candy-mob — the world doesn't change, only the protagonist did. No speech naming the year of humiliation.
