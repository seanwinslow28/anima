## What is this story about?

A small, timid kid is humiliated at a spoiled birthday girl's backyard party: the bully host dad calls him up for "first licks" at the piñata, then yanks the string mid-swing. Face-plant; the kids roar. The kid's grandmother — his best friend, who lived in his house — died a few weeks earlier. He comes home wrecked and, by lifelong reflex, walks to her room for comfort — and hits the absence. In a box he's never seen: her worn red headband (a small phoenix stitched on it) and a faded 1970s photo of her young, mid-flying-kick, holding a ninja star — she was secretly a real martial artist. On the photo's back, her decades-old handwriting: "The phoenix is born when it *rises*." He plays a VHS to feel close to her — a home movie of the games they played when he was little, her boombox song underneath, her laugh on the degraded mic. The tape glitches: taped over beneath is her 1970s training footage — the same moves. She'd been secretly training him his whole life, disguised as play. He ties on the headband and trains for a year, entirely his own choice, scored by her song. A year later, Brittany's next-birthday invitation arrives in his mailbox; he returns, snatches the tossed stick from the air, and mid-leap severs the piñata string with her one ninja star. The piñata erupts a geyser of colorful liquid. In the stunned silence he walks away; one pristine lollipop arcs out and lands in his path; he plucks it mid-stride — first and last licks. The bully dad is muted, flashes involuntary respect, shakes it off, learns nothing. The kid walks home as an echo of the training music rises. The world didn't change; only he did. A tribute wrapped in a genre-collision comedy.

## Who are the characters?

**The Kid** — smallest at the party; too-big square glasses, shoulders in, messy brown hair, chunky worn sneakers (design locked, 2026-07-14 sprint). Glasses = shed armor: wimpy = glasses + no headband; trained = fitted headband + no glasses. **Unnamed on screen, deliberately** — the world never learns his name. One Act-1 competence plant: something whips at his head and his hand snaps up and catches it without looking; he startles at his own hand; everyone glosses it as fluke.

**The Late Grandmother** — died weeks before Act 1; lived in the kid's house. Never on screen as a living character. Carried by: her room (kung-fu posters, tape shelf, Polaroids-through-the-years as set-dressing texture), the box (headband + 1970s warrior photo + inscription + a second compartment holding her real ninja star, found mid-montage), the taped-over VHS (home movie of their disguised-training games over her 1970s footage), and **one laugh** in the home-movie audio — the only voice she ever gets. She never speaks a word and leaves no message addressed to him. She secretly trained him through their games; the reveal shows preparation, never assignment; the year of training is entirely his own choice.

**Brittany, the birthday girl** — sly bratty princess (Angelica Pickles / Regina George species); invited the kid as a setup; opens presents mid-party tossing cards unread; Act 3 = her next birthday; first into the candy mob, never punished, never changed. Named only by the end-card tape label.

**The Host Dad — the bully.** A bigger, douchier male version of Brittany (design: family resemblance). Hates weak people; the piñata string is his weapon (he yanks it mid-swing, deliberately). Says the title line once: "let's give this little freak back there first licks." At the disarm: muted, pokes the star in the fence, one involuntary flash of respect, then shakes it off in body language. **Never punished beyond the disarm, never learns, never apologizes.**

**The Mom** — wordless background sly hostess; curates the party for appearances (rotates the cake to its good side; engineered the invite-the-whole-class). Visual similarity to Brittany.

**The camera-holder** — faceless adult voice behind the Act-1 camcorder POV; one line ("Wh--... When did Linda get the sad clown statue?"); **never seen** (fallback ladder: silhouette → neck-down partial → cut the startle beat; never a face).

**The kid's parents** — exist, functionally absent: implied, heard, or silhouetted at most. Never load-bearing.

**Extras with jobs** — the neighbor-over-the-fence (the montage's single runner: confused → curious → invested → one imperceptible nod, never seen again); the solemn 7am mailman nod (sacred one-off; the party-2 invitation arrives via the mailbox WITHOUT him); the grill-tong griller (cold-open one-off). Party kids/adults are generation guidance, not designed principals.

## What is the tone?

Sincere, with a dark comic edge — samurai-cinema gravity welded onto a suburban backyard birthday party; comedy only from that collision. Genndy Tartakovsky homage: timing-as-a-song, hold-and-burst, stylized non-naturalistic "blood." Pixar-caliber emotional showing with adult-skewing, darker-than-Pixar background comedy. **~3:30–4:00, cold open + three acts.**

### What this is NOT

- Not glossy, not anime-clean, not 3D-rendered.
- Not saccharine — no eulogy, no funeral, no deathbed, no hospital, no speech about the loss, no spoken word from the grandmother, ever. Her laugh (once, in the home-movie audio) is the only voice she gets.
- Not a "tough grandma" cutout — she's a specific person, carried by artifacts.
- Not mean toward the kid — the collision is the joke, never him; the world's cruelty is casual (erasure), not gleeful, except the dad's.
- Not a revenge movie — he trains on a burlap sack/tire, never a piñata effigy; the return is a *response to an invitation*, not a plan; the star targets the string, never the man; the "why did he train" answer is "for her / for himself."
- Not a comeuppance movie — the dad is a bully and the piece knows it, but he is never punished beyond the disarm and never learns; the world doesn't change, only the protagonist did.
- Not a magic-backstory shortcut — she prepared him without his knowing, but the reveal is evidence he finds himself, never instruction; no message addressed to him exists.
- Not wacky — every joke is earned, grounded, human (the Big Gulps rule); random ≠ zany.

## What are the non-negotiables?

- **Timing is a song:** move → dead-stop hold → burst; two-band hold spec (ruled 2026-07-19): in-action anticipation holds 2–4s past comfort; the designated hero holds (cold-open push, pre-strike standoff, post-geyser silence, final replica wide) 6–10s. Buildup in seconds, the strike in frames. Comedy uses the same holds.
- **Register:** `primal-sketch-grit`, LOCKED 2026-07-15 (registered in `pipeline/registers.py`; gpt-image transport via Higgsfield). Two deliberate cross-register staging borrows, each a feasibility-flagged single choice: the candy-liquid geyser (Samurai Jack oil-for-blood logic) and ONE `90s-nicktoon-grossout` gross-up closeup of the bootleg piñata's face. The 4:3 VHS/camcorder look is a shared overlay treatment, not a register.
- **The Act-2 turn is a strict no-joke zone.**
- **Voice rules:** the grandmother never speaks and leaves no message addressed to him — her laugh, once, inside the home-movie audio, is her only voice. The inscription is hers to herself, decades old ("The phoenix is born when it *rises*.", rise underlined). No ghost-beat — it was cut 2026-07-18; do not reintroduce her reflection/apparition in any form.
- **The instilling engine:** the home-movie games and the 1970s footage show THE SAME MOVES — the glitch match-cut must carry "she was secretly training him through their games" wordlessly (verification gate: a silent animatic must produce that sentence from cold viewers unprompted; if it needs narration, the design fails).
- **The Act-3 star staging:** the star targets the STRING; the dad's hand is never in the sever frame; the flight line composes toward tree/piñata; the throw reads as liberating the piñata, never attacking the man. Cold-read gate before this beat's board locks: 5 stills, silent, narrate-back; weapon-at-person words ("threw a knife at the dad") = restage.
- **Act-1 cruelty calibration:** kids roar; adults wince or chuckle awkwardly; the dad is the only adult enjoying it.
- **The geyser:** colorful LIQUID only — candy implied, no solid pieces on screen; oil-spill flow; over the dad's shoulder while he faces the wrong way. Exception: ONE pristine lollipop arcs out and lands upright in the kid's exit path (the title's payoff).
- **The ending guardrails:** the kid never looks back; no glance-up beat; the tribute button is audio (an echo of the training music slowly rising — no laugh in the exit audio); the dad gets the involuntary respect-flash then the in-character shake-off; Brittany is first into the mob, unchanged; end card = the "BRITTANY'S 9th — DO NOT TAPE OVER" cassette, taped over anyway.
- **The headband:** phoenix stitched small; too-big at the start, fits by the return; visible in every training beat and the return. The kid stays recognizably himself in every frame.
- **Comedy doctrine:** placement map (Act 1 background one-offs / turn = none / montage physical + the one neighbor runner / Act 3 silence gags only); Big Gulps rule for one-offs; the mailman nod is sacred and single; no clowns anywhere except the Linda line; the kill list in the writers'-room doc §6 is binding — do not regenerate killed material.
- **Runtime honesty:** stopwatch table-read of the beat sheet against 3:30–4:00 at the Sam/Bea stage; the montage trims first; the turn never does.
- **Music:** the boombox song is the only music in the film (degraded-diegetic → montage arrangement → exit echo); everything else is silence + single-foley. The temp track must exist before hold durations lock.
- **Audio is a human-owned post pass** on the assembled cut (no pipeline audio stage); dialogue is Sean-performed via ElevenLabs Voice Changer; the camera-holder startle is generated in two takes (natural startled noise vs. sharp inhale + camera yank) and decided in the edit.
