## Title

**FIRST LICKS** — locked 2026-07-18. The bully dad's own humiliation line ("let's give this little freak first licks"), reclaimed. Four meanings: called up first / getting your licks in / the exit lollipop / first *and last* licks. The line stays diegetic exactly once (the Act-1 call-up). Register rule that produced it: a flat, mundane phrase that detonates a second meaning after the film. No other "lick"-adjacent dialogue anywhere. *(Former options GRANDMASTER / HER STRIKE / THE HEADBAND / LAST TAPE retired; full 68-title record in the writers'-room doc §7.)*

## Logline

Weeks after losing his grandmother — his best friend — a timid kid humiliated at a birthday-party piñata ritual discovers she was secretly a martial-arts master who'd been training him his whole life, disguised as the games they played. He trains for a year in her memory and returns to the same party to take the piñata down like a small samurai.

## Register

Sincere, with a dark comic edge. A **genre collision**: the emotional gravity and staging of samurai cinema welded onto a suburban backyard birthday party. Comedy comes only from that collision — never from mocking the kid. A homage to **Genndy Tartakovsky**: timing-as-a-song, hold-and-burst, stylized non-naturalistic "blood." Visual register **LOCKED 2026-07-15: `primal-sketch-grit`** (the cartoon-pushed, web-search-grounded look; the samurai-jack-s5 A/B is closed — see [`go-no-go.md`](go-no-go.md)).

**Runtime: ~3:30–4:00** (revised 2026-07-18 from "~2–3 minutes" — the red-team's beat-timed math showed the built story is a 4:30–6:00 film at natural pacing; the honest target protects the Act-2 turn instead of starving it. A stopwatch table-read against the beat sheet is mandatory at the Sam/Bea stage; if it exceeds ~4:00, the montage trims first, the turn never does.)

## The cast

- **The Kid** — smallest at the party, timid; too-big square glasses, shoulders in, messy brown hair, chunky worn sneakers (design locked, 2026-07-14 sprint). **Glasses = shed armor**: wimpy state wears glasses + no headband; trained state wears the fitted headband + no glasses. **Deliberately unnamed on screen** — the dad calls him "freak," the camera-voice calls him a statue; the world never bothers to learn his name (the erasure species, made structural). Production id: `kid`.
- **The Late Grandmother — the engine.** Died a few **weeks** before Act 1. Lived in the kid's house; his best friend and comfort person; they watched kung-fu movies together weekly. Never on screen as a living character — carried by her room, the box, the tape, one photograph, and **one laugh** (see Voice Rules). Two artifact looks (designed 2026-07-14): the warm photo-with-the-kid, and the 1970s flying-kick reveal snapshot — unmistakably one woman.
- **Brittany, the birthday girl** — Angelica-Pickles / Regina-George species: raised a princess, sly, acts friendly, sets people up for her own entertainment. **She invited the kid as a setup.** Act 3 = her next birthday. Never punished, never changed — first into the candy mob. Named only by the end-card tape label.
- **The Host Dad — the bully.** A bigger, douchier male version of Brittany (family resemblance is a design note). Hates weak people; puts them down and calls it a learning experience, because that's how he was raised (bible only — never screen time). Runs the piñata ritual; the string is *his weapon* (he yanks it mid-swing, deliberately). **Never punished beyond the disarm, never learns, never apologizes** — see Decision Record #4.
- **The Mom** — background sly hostess. Wordless. Curates the party for appearances (the camcorder finds her rotating the cake to its good side; she made Brittany invite the whole class — more presents, no grief at school meetings). Visual similarity to Brittany.
- **The camera-holder** — a faceless adult voice behind the Act-1 camcorder POV. **Never seen** (fallback ladder if the never-seen startle fails in motion: silhouette → neck-down partial → cut the startle beat; never a face). One line only.
- **The kid's parents** — exist, functionally absent: implied, heard, or silhouetted at most (montage latitude). Never load-bearing. Nobody else in that house noticed what he lost.
- **Extras with jobs:** the neighbor-over-the-fence (the montage's one runner), the solemn mailman (sacred one-off), the grill-tong griller, party kids/adults (generation guidance, not designed principals).

## The engine — preparation, not instruction (the amended core)

**She'd been secretly training him his whole life, disguised as play.** The games they played — blocking her hand-slaps patty-cake style, balancing the fence, catching what she tossed — were forms. He never knew. *He* discovers it, alone, through evidence:

1. **The reflex** — humiliated, he comes home and does what he's always done after a bad day: walks down the hall to her room for comfort. The reflex hits the absence. That's why he's in her room; he went looking for his person and found the warrior.
2. **The box** (something he's never seen, peeking out) — her worn red headband (small **phoenix** stitched on it) and the 1970s photo: her, young, mid-flying-kick, **holding a ninja star**, unmistakably a real martial artist. She was *small too* — the proof it's possible for someone built like him.
3. **The inscription** — on the photo's back, her handwriting, decades old, written for herself, **never addressed to him**: ***"The phoenix is born when it <u>rises</u>."*** (her underline). Cold read: her creed. After the tape: the film's thesis. At the money shot: a caption the audience read forty seconds in without knowing it.
4. **The tape** — he presses play *to finish the ritual* (movie night was the comfort; she's gone, the ritual isn't). A home movie of them playing their games, her boombox song playing, **her laugh on the degraded mic**. The tape **glitches**: taped over underneath is her 1970s training footage — *the same moves*. Match cut: his childhood game / her warrior form. The inference detonates in him, not on screen.

**The amendment, stated plainly** (revises the 2026-07-02 core "the reveal is evidence, not instruction; the training is entirely his own choice"): *she prepared him without his knowing; the reveal shows preparation, never assignment. The choice to train — and everything in the year — stays entirely his. She never speaks to him and leaves him no message addressed to him, ever.* The literal-coaching version (a VHS of her teaching him) was considered and **killed** — it breaks discovery. The agency engine survives; the loneliness of the choice softens into inheritance.

**The competence plant (Act 1, required):** early in the party, something whips at the kid's head and his hand *snaps up and catches it without looking* — he stares at his own hand, startled, drops it, shuffles off; everyone glosses it as a fluke. Installed reflexes, planted before the reveal explains them. Without this plant the reveal contradicts the fumbling kid we watched (red-team finding, adopted).

**The star's origin:** the box's **second compartment**, found mid-montage — her real ninja star, the same one visible in the 1970s photo (never mentioned; freeze-frame reward). Everything he needed was already hers; he supplies only the year. No forging.

**ACT 3 — The Return + The Strike.** *(Choreography canonized 2026-07-20 — DR #19; the 07-18 brief misread the weapon.)* Same backyard, same ritual, escalated rentals in identical framings (party-2 world rule). The headband fits. The dad calls the trained kid up and **tosses the stick at him again — the exact mirror of party 1 — and this time the kid snatches it out of the air without looking, staring the dad down.** The **Kevin-McCallister beat**: he came prepared, one step ahead. He **poses with the stick, ready to strike; charges; leaps, both hands on the stick.** The dad reaches for his signature cheat — the string-yank meant to face-plant the kid again. **Mid-air, in one swift motion, the kid draws grandma's ninja star from his pocket with his free hand (the other stays on the stick) and throws it: it severs the pull-string right where the dad grips it, the dad recoils and lets go, and the star flies on to lodge in the fence.** The knot stays tied to the branch, so the piñata **holds its place.** Then the kid **STRIKES the piñata with the stick** — the stick is the katana; the star only foiled the sabotage. **FLASH** — to black, or to the kids' faces frozen in awe — over the sound of the kid landing.

**Staging non-negotiable (amended DR #19):** two weapons, two distinct jobs — **the STICK strikes and splits the piñata; the STAR severs the pull-string.** The star **targets the string, never the man's body**; it cuts the string at the dad's grip so he releases, then lodges in the fence — reading as *foiling his cheat*, never as an attack on him. The star is deliberately the **comic extreme** (a ninja weapon at a kids' party) and the *came-prepared* inversion of party 1. The **star-staging cold read (gate 3) is now MORE load-bearing**: the stills must read "cut the string / stopped the cheat," never "threw a blade at the man."

**Signature framing (DR #18 + #19):** we cut back **TIGHT on the kid** — his face bottom-right of frame, eyes off-camera, held in an **extreme post-strike samurai pose with the stick in frame** — the **piñata intact over his shoulder.** Then it **splits open at the bottom** and the **rainbow geyser floods down** — candy implied, liquid only, no solid pieces (the Samurai-Jack oil-for-blood convention, deliberately borrowed cross-register) — while the kid **holds the pose without moving a muscle** (the intact→flooding beat is a start/end motion pair; his stillness is the whole point). Cut to an **extreme wide of the whole scene: the party kids ring it as awed silhouettes, the dad shocked, the kid still holding the pose.** *(The dad-facing-away OTS survives as exploratory coverage — spatial staging corrected per DR #19: the dad on the OPPOSITE side of the yard, camera flipped, full daylight, never a darkened silhouette.)* Long held wide of stunned silence (silence-gag lineup: the flash-flinch inversion — everyone flinches at a camera flash except the exiting kid; patient-zero — the drenched kid licks his own cheek, verdict registers, mob begins; Sparkle Horse's severed face landing in the potato salad). Then the kids **charge the candy geyser, Brittany first, still awful.** The kid **stands up straight, spins the stick once, and walks away — confident, proud, no look back.** Cut to the muted dad **staring at the ninja star lodged in the fence, one involuntary flash of respect, then the "yea… whatever" shake-off** — he learns nothing, the world doesn't change. At the gate, **a lollipop bounces his way out of the chaos; he snatches it from the air, unwraps it, pops it in his mouth** — first and last licks — and heads home (toward the house where she lived), **the stick resting on his shoulder,** as **an echo of the training music slowly rises**. *(No glance-up beat; the tribute button is audio. No laugh in the exit audio — warm-in-context reads creepy out-of-context; final ending sound design is settled at the audio pass.)* **End card:** the camcorder cassette labeled "BRITTANY'S 9th — DO NOT TAPE OVER." Next year's party is taped over it anyway. The family does to their own tape what time did to grandma's.

## Voice rules (locked)

- The camera-holder gets one line (the Linda line — the erasure species: lines that don't register the kid as a person cut deeper than lines naming his sadness; alternates + species ruling in the writers'-room doc §5).

- The kid: near-silent (any lines are Sam's proposal, Sean's gate).

## The Genndy timing bible

1. **Timing is a song** — quiet parts and loud parts. The humiliation is the quiet; the return is the loud.
2. **Move → dead-stop HOLD → BURST.** Two-band hold spec (F1 ruling, 2026-07-19): in-action anticipation holds run **2–4s** past comfort; the **designated hero holds** — cold-open push, pre-strike standoff, post-geyser silence, final replica wide — run **6–10s** (the measured Genndy dread band). Buildup in seconds, the strike in frames.
3. **Raw Primal-register linework** — visible, weight-varying hand-drawn ink, gritty painterly texture, warm earthy desaturated palette; figure and background share one treatment (`primal-sketch-grit`, locked).
4. **Per-shot letterbox** — cold open + peak-tension beats are the anchored uses; other scenes may earn it case-by-case (Bea proposes at boards, Sean gates; F8 ruling, 2026-07-19). Never the base format, never a semantic memory marker (the 4:3 inset owns memory).
5. **Montage** — short escalating one-skill-per-beat vignettes, repetition-with-variation, near-dialogue-free.
6. **Comedy = the same holds** — deadpan hold-then-reaction; drop the sound then snap it back; smash-cut a punchline at its peak.
7. **Passage of time = object decay + palette rotation** (the boombox calendar; the gi-to-crest principle).

31. **Art-style anti-drift — the NO-CHAIN-EDIT law (standing production rule — EVERY anima short on the gpt-image / ChatGPT path).** Batch M2 drifted its **register** mid-run: the piece is `primal-sketch-grit` (HEAVY bold weight-varying near-black ink kept OVER the color, gritty painterly masses, stark value contrast), but `yard-front-drive` / `petting-zoo` / (mildly) `usedcar-lot` came back as a **"graphic-novel" look — thin uniform cross-hatched pen-and-ink + a muted watercolor wash** (architectural-illustration style), the opposite of Primal. **Diagnosis (confirmed by side-by-side view + census):** *chain-edit generation loss* — gpt-image re-renders the whole frame on every edit and regresses toward its clean-illustrative default a little more each pass, so the re-edits that **corrected the plates' setting** (fixing palm/desert/stucco → NJ) simultaneously **eroded the bold ink into thin hatching** — the setting-fix and the style-drift were the same edits pulling opposite ways (front-drive, the most re-edited, drifted worst). The load-bearing counter-example: `yard-home-endsummer`, a *single* clean edit of the on-register master, held bold and gritty — proving flat-daylight environments are NOT the cause. **The law:** (a) **NO CHAIN-EDITING** — each plate is generated FRESH or a SINGLE edit off a CLEAN on-register source, never an edit of an edit; to fix setting/season/light, re-edit the clean source, not the drifted output; a drifted plate is discarded, never re-edited. (b) **`KEEPER` style-match anchor** — every fresh environment plate feeds an on-register keeper (`yard-home-endsummer.png`) as an *art-style-only* reference (the reference carries the register more reliably than prose; technique-kit §a/§b). (c) **Anti-drift clause is positive-led** — name the target in the affirmative + a short render-quality negation ("not thin, not uniform, not a wash"); do NOT enumerate loaded mediums as negatives (watercolor / cross-hatch / graphic-novel), since naming a look as a negative can *evoke* it (register research §1). (d) **Per-batch consistency check** in the orchestration — compare each plate to the keeper before showing; if the line went thin/uniform or a wash appeared, regenerate fresh. Fix landed in [`montage-prompt-pack-v2.md`](../../runs/2026-07-19-first-licks-artdept/montage-prompt-pack-v2.md) + [`montage-orchestration-v2.md`](../../runs/2026-07-19-first-licks-artdept/montage-orchestration-v2.md); M2 regenerates all three fresh, and the M3 composites built on the drifted BGs (groceries + crane → front-drive, donkey → petting-zoo, tube-man → used-car-lot) re-run off the fixed plates.
