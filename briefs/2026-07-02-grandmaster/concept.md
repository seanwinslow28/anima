## Title (options — lean first)

- **GRANDMASTER** — my lean. Grandmother *and* kung-fu grand master in one word; sincere and funny, and it's the whole premise in a single noun.
- **HER STRIKE** — doubles as his year of training and the string he finally cuts; keeps the tribute inside the title itself.
- **THE HEADBAND** — the plainest, most literal option; leans on the one object that carries the whole arc.
- **LAST TAPE** — names the spark specifically (the unfinished VHS); moodier, less immediately legible as comedy.

## Logline

A grieving, timid kid trains for a year in the kung-fu movies his late grandmother left behind — and in the discovery that she was secretly a warrior — so he can return to the birthday party that humiliated him and take down the piñata like a small samurai.

## Register

Sincere, with a dark comic edge. A **genre collision**: the emotional gravity and staging of a samurai film welded onto a suburban backyard birthday party. Comedy is engineered to come only from that collision — never from mocking the kid, and (this session's correction to the obvious reading) never from mocking the host dad either. A homage to **Genndy Tartakovsky** (*Samurai Jack*): timing-as-a-song, silhouette-and-hold, stylized non-naturalistic "blood." **~2–3 minutes, three acts.**

## The character + the engine

- **The kid** — the smallest at the party, timid, with a specific silhouette-readable tell: shoulders in, avoids eye contact, glasses a size too big for his face. Not a loser — something coiled underneath. Wears his late grandmother's **faded headband**, the load-bearing continuity object: too-big-swallowing-his-head at the start, fitting by the return.
- **The late grandmother — the engine.** She passed. Never appears as a living character. Present only through the room (kung-fu-movie posters, an old TV, a framed photo of the two of them), the headband, one unfinished VHS tape, and a single faded snapshot: her, *young and mid-flying-kick*, fierce and unmistakable — the reveal that she was quietly a warrior.
- **Whose choice the training is.** He finds the photo himself, in the box of her things. He presses play on the unfinished tape himself. The spark catches, and after that the year is entirely his own doing — not her instruction, not a lesson she left him. The reveal is evidence, not a claim; it proves the capability was real and leaves the choice to him.
- **The restraint.** No dialogue from her, ever — only objects, the room, and one photograph. **One ghost-beat maximum:** her reflection catching in the dark TV glass for a second while he ties the headband on, then gone. She stays a specific person, never a "tough grandma" cutout.
- **The host dad.** Runs the piñata ritual — calls the kid up, tosses the stick, works the string. Not a villain; the unwitting fulcrum of the reversal. At the disarm, his reaction reads as **"the ritual itself just got weird,"** aimed at the situation, not at personal defeat — preceded by one half-beat of real respect (his hand freezes on the cut string, he looks at the kid) before he defaults to bewildered horror at the ninja star. The piece's no-mockery rule protects him exactly as it protects the kid.

## Structure — three acts

**Act 1 — The Humiliation (~40s).** Backyard birthday party, kids at play. The host dad gathers everyone at the tree and calls the timid kid up first. He tosses the stick — the kid fumbles it (or it bonks him); everyone laughs. The kid charges, jumps — the dad tugs the string, the piñata rises, the kid face-plants. Bigger laugh. A bratty kid nearby: *"I'm totally getting one of these for my party."* The kid lies in the grass as the laughter distorts and echoes. Something changes behind his eyes.

**Act 2 — The Turn + The Year (~70s).** Cut to his room: kung-fu posters, an old TV, a framed photo of him and his grandma. He opens a box of her things — the faded headband on a stack of worn VHS tapes, and beneath it, the snapshot of *young Grandma mid-flying-kick*. Ghost-beat: her reflection in the dark screen as he ties the headband on; it slips over his eyes; he pushes it up. He presses play on the unfinished tape — his own hand, his own choice — and the montage that follows is entirely his: one skill per vignette, repetition-with-variation, near-dialogue-free, score-driven, a calendar marking the year between cuts. He grows into the headband.

**Act 3 — The Return + The Strike (~60s).** One year later — same backyard, same host dad, same ritual. The headband fits now. The dad tosses the stick — the kid **snatches it from the air** without looking. The full samurai-cut sequence: he charges, jumps; the dad starts to tug the string; mid-air the kid draws a *real* ninja star and throws — it **severs the string from the dad's hand**. The piñata drops. The dad's hand freezes on the cut string — one half-beat of real respect as he looks at the kid — before "the ritual just got weird" takes over: he stares at the star, bewildered, not humiliated. Slow-mo → quick flash → low angle: the kid lands in striking follow-through, piñata behind him. **Hold — past comfort.** Then the piñata's belly erupts: candy sprays like a stylized geyser. The kids stare, then mob the stream — and in the mob is the same bratty kid from Act 1, still oblivious, already grabbing candy and saying something equally self-absorbed. The world didn't notice or change; only he did. The kid plucks a lollipop, walks out — a private glance up, for her. No words.

## The Genndy style + timing bible

1. **Timing is a song** — quiet parts and loud parts. The humiliation is the quiet; the return is the loud.
2. **Move → dead-stop HOLD → BURST.** Hold the pre-strike frame 2–4 seconds past comfort; the buildup is measured in seconds, the strike in frames.
3. **Raw, visible ink linework carries the line** — sketchy, hand-drawn, weight-varying strokes, not a clean flat silhouette. *(Superseded 2026-07-03, [L16]: the piece's ART-VIZ pick is Tartakovsky's* Primal *register, not* Samurai Jack*'s flat-silhouette one — the two are mutually exclusive, not two readings of the same rule.)*
4. **Gritty, painterly texture over a warm, earthy, desaturated palette** — raw and physical, not clean or polished; figure and background share the same hand-drawn treatment.
5. **Flat, angular characters over richly painted backgrounds** — force non-naturalistic palettes per location.
6. **Per-shot letterbox** for peak-tension beats only — not the base format.
7. **Montage** — short escalating one-skill-per-beat vignettes, repetition-with-variation, near-dialogue-free.
8. **Comedy = the same holds** — deadpan hold-then-reaction; drop the sound then snap it back; smash-cut a punchline at its peak.

*(Craft research: Rotten Tomatoes "5 Ways Genndy Tartakovsky Revolutionized Animated Action"; Animation Obsessive's Samurai Jack pieces; canmom.art "Animation Night 35"; TV Tropes' Samurai Jack blood-substitution convention; AWN's Tartakovsky interview on Primal.)*

## The candy mechanic (the money shot)

Tartakovsky fought **robots** in *Samurai Jack* specifically so he could show "blood" on a kids' network — enemies spew oil, sparks, and goo, never red. **The piñata is this piece's robot.** Stage the candy burst exactly like that oil-geyser: over-saturated, non-naturalistic, arterial-scale, cut on the moment of impact. It can spill its guts in bright geometric candy *because it isn't blood* — the button the whole short builds toward.

*Craft-lineage correction (2026-07-03, from the `primal-sketch-grit` register research):* the oil-for-blood substitution above is **Samurai Jack's** convention, not *Primal*'s — Primal's own convention is explicit, shock-colored blood. The candy-geyser stays exactly as staged: it is a **deliberate cross-register staging choice** (Jack's blood-substitution gag performed inside the `primal-sketch-grit` register), not a feature of the register itself, and the register spec does not claim it. Story and staging unchanged; only the attribution is corrected.

## Stress test (pre-mortem + red-team + verdict)

*Run 2026-07-03 — Step 2.75, the STRESS-TEST stage. An in-room reflex ran first (a same-context warm-up, not the authority — it surfaced nothing beyond the already-named threads below). The authoritative pass was a fresh-context reviewer that read only this concept doc + the Studio Brief, blind to the session sidecar and blind to the room's own history — genuine independence from the love story that built this piece.*

The concept's protected core — the dual sincerity-and-collision-comedy objective, delivered through evidence-not-instruction and symmetric no-mockery restraint toward the kid, the dad, and the grandmother alike — was steelmanned and attacked. It held. Sound as specified; the pass did not touch it.

Two real, falsifiable risks surfaced, both Launch-Blocking for Cy/Bea (not front-door-blocking):

- **The ninja-star/host-dad beat has no staged safety valve.** The candy-as-oil "blood substitution" logic covers the piñata strike; it does not cover the separate action of a child throwing a real edged weapon near an adult's actively-gripping hand. **Fails if** a cold read of just that beat (wind-up → release → flight path → impact → the dad's hand/face) registers as "the kid attacked the dad" rather than "a movie flourish." **Cheapest test:** 4–5 stills, shown cold, one question — what just happened, and how do you feel about the dad. More than 1-in-5 alarmed reads means redirecting the star's trajectory away from the hand's vicinity.
- **The register-extension decision ([L17]) has no owner, deadline, or fallback trigger.** Resolving it is currently an aspiration, not a decision procedure. **Fails if** the Primal-grit spike is attempted mid-Bible-pass with no pre-agreed fallback to Route C. **Cheapest test:** one single-frame spike shown against Route C's already-buildable prompt, with an explicit go/no-go recorded before Cy's pass starts.

**Verdict: revise.** Sean's call, 2026-07-03 — clear both items above before Cy's Bible pass locks; lightweight, not a re-brainstorm. Both residuals are production-binding and fold into `00_studio_brief.md`'s non-negotiables — Maya, Sam, and Bea read only that file, so a Launch-Blocking Tiger living here alone would be invisible to planning.
