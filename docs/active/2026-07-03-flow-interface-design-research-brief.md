## The through-lines (read these first)

**1. You cannot judge animation from a still — and the current eye-gate shows a still.** Every professional animation and dailies tool treats *flipping/rocking* as sacred: the illusion of motion only appears in the rock between frames (TVPaint's dedicated Flip panel; RoughAnimator's drag-to-flip; Frame.io's JKL). The single biggest gap in the prototype eye-gate is that it presents one static candidate. The fix is a **hold-key that rocks the loop** (or plays it) plus a one-key **lights-out** toggle that drops all chrome — the two-key judging ritual. This is the highest-leverage change in the whole brief, and it's free/UI-only (the daemon already serves the frames).

**2. The fast review loop is one keystroke, and the tool infers the rest.** Frame.io + SyncSketch converge on a rhythm anima half-has: *see → rock → verdict-key → auto-advance*, where (a) the decision key **itself** advances to the next unreviewed frame, (b) starting to **type a retry auto-pauses** the loop (no separate stop), and (c) traversal **skips already-approved frames**. anima's Enter/R is in the groove; the win is guaranteeing Enter *advances* and typing *auto-pauses*.

**3. anima already owns the anti-template answer Flow and ComfyUI are groping toward.** Flow feels like a template because it leads with the prompt box, hides cost and critique, buries you in a thumbnail grid, and personifies nothing (Chase Jarvis, Android Police reviews). ComfyUI's node graph is powerful but illegible, and every fix is *progressive disclosure* (hide the graph, show a linear spine). anima's counters are structural, not cosmetic: **lead with state + cost + the critic's read**, keep the DAG hidden behind the **run-state stepper** (`next_action` literally is "where am I / what's next"), and give the pipeline **named faces**. Don't dilute these — they're the differentiator.

**4. Warmth is motion + instant response, not texture.** Procreate Dreams is the existence proof for "tactile without twee": its warmth is 100% haptics, gesture, earned metaphor, and sub-frame responsiveness — and 0% skeuomorphic decoration (no wood-grain, no paper-tear SFX, no page-curl). Linear/Superhuman say the same from the pro-tool side: sub-100ms feedback *is* the craft. For anima this means the **cel-flip + instant keyboard response + the warm/dark seam ARE the entire tactility budget** — the warm-paper `#FBF5E9` chrome supplies "paper" as a calm surface; never add a faux-paper texture to the stage.

**5. The dark stage has real perceptual science — and one honest caveat.** The black box (dark gallery wall) was invented precisely for luminous/moving subjects — a pencil-test *loop* is exactly that subject. Simultaneous contrast means a dark surround makes the frame read brighter and "presented" (great for reverence and focus). The caveat: dark *lifts* perceived brightness, so it subtly lies about palette. Keep `#15161A` as default; offer an optional **neutral-gray color-check mat** for the moments palette accuracy is judged (which maps to Em's palette style-class).

**6. The museum is a solved pattern, and anima already has the ingredients.** Google Arts & Culture (7,000+ exhibits) proves the shape: a curated **linear** narrative, one work at a time, from a repeatable template. The dominant mechanic is **sticky-pin scrollytelling** (pin the frame, scroll the docent story past it) with **before/after sliders** as "tangible proof of transformation" — which map 1:1 onto anima's draft→pro and retry-ladder decisions. anima has the exhibit schema, Mo the docent, and a static-site generator already; the research says wire them into a *pinned, comparison-driven, decision-moment-curated* walkthrough, not a browse grid.

## Tensions for Sean (recommend-with-a-lean; you pick in Stage 2)

- **The two reds.** Onion-skin convention is "previous = red, next = green," but anima's *signature* is the red grease-pencil critic mark. Two reds must never share the stage. **Lean:** keep grease-red for the critic (signature is sacred); render onion "previous" as a desaturated cool tint and onion "next" as `teal-bright`, staying inside the locked palette and preserving warm-behind/cool-ahead logic without a second red.
- **Does the dark stage lie about color?** Simultaneous contrast says yes, slightly (dark lifts perceived brightness). **Lean:** dark `#15161A` stays the default for reverence/focus; add an optional neutral-gray color-check mat for palette verdicts — it directly serves Em's palette style-class.
- **Loop-by-default preview.** Every NLE defaults to play-once and buries loop; anima ships a *loop*. **Lean:** diverge — loop the whole assembled sequence by default; the seam (last frame → frame 1) is always under evaluation, which is literally Engine Truth.
- **How personified does the crew get?** **Lean:** honest role-personas only — role + a consistent restrained voice, opinions about *the work*, never feelings about Sean or itself (the Clippy/Replika failure mode; one cited study puts the trust cost of over-anthropomorphism at ~47%, single-source, treat as directional not gospel). Restraint is the anti-cringe control, and anima's Newsreader/JetBrains-Mono wire-service register already is it.
- **Grid vs eye-gate default mid-generate.** (Open question already in the spec.) Research leans the spec's way: **eye-gate on `review_frame`, the generate grid on demand** — the grid is the survey, the eye-gate is the work.

## Category findings

### A · Professional 2D animation software

*Studied: Toon Boom Harmony + Storyboard Pro, TVPaint, Procreate Dreams (hardest), Callipeg, Storyboarder, Boords, RoughAnimator, OpenToonz, Cavalry, Rive.*

- **Vertical x-sheet: one row per frame, the drawing *named* in the cell, header pinned.** Timing is read down a column; the cell is an index, not the art. *Steal:* the reel's spine — frame number + status + ID in JetBrains Mono, a thumbnail toggle (Harmony's "Show Thumbnails") for when you want the picture. *Avoid:* an always-on filmstrip (the cliché that fights the calm chrome). — Toon Boom Learn Xsheet; OpenToonz docs.
- **Held-frame duration as a first-class, editable object.** Procreate Dreams: tap-hold → type a number, or drag "Free Trim" handles, to make "twos and fours"; Harmony sets holds with `Ctrl+1/2/3/4`. *Steal:* number keys set the selected frame's hold in the reel + a drag handle; show a hold as a taller/merged cell. anima's `holds.json` already exists — this is UI over existing truth. — Procreate Dreams handbook; Harmony shortcuts.
- **Onion-skin: red = previous, green = next, per-frame opacity falloff, silhouette-vs-image toggle.** Color = time-direction pre-attentively; Callipeg's "Colors" (tinted silhouette) mode is the *placement-check* that pairs with anima's Phase-4 silhouette philosophy. *Steal:* a 1-2-before/after ghost with graduated opacity; resolve the red collision (see Tensions). *Avoid:* the full 8-peg apparatus (iPad real estate a desktop tool shouldn't spend). — Toon Boom onion-skin; Callipeg.
- **Procreate Dreams "Flipbook" + earned metaphor + haptic/gesture warmth.** Swipe to advance (faster swipe = faster), pink current-frame highlight, four-finger-tap = full-screen preview, squeeze-Pencil = play/pause. Warmth is entirely sensory-motor. *Steal:* the earned-metaphor discipline (the cel-flip already is this), the single strong current-frame highlight, and the instant full-screen toggle (top-15 #1). *Avoid:* illustrating the metaphor (spiral binding, thumb-tabs). — Procreate Dreams gestures/flipbook.
- **Flip as a named, first-class tool.** TVPaint's Flip panel rocks between *marked key drawings only*, hand-controlling speed by pen movement. *Steal:* a "flip" hold-key that rocks the loop, and "step only between approved anchors" vs "all candidates." — TVPaint Flip panel.
- **Single-frame-under-scrutiny: the Panel view.** Storyboard Pro puts the selected panel large with **structured caption fields** (Dialogue/Action/Slugging/Notes) beside it. *Steal:* this is the eye-gate — the dark stage with Em's fields (Verdict/Reasoning/Proposed-fix/Cites) as fixed labeled slots (top-15 #5). — Storyboard Pro Panel view.

*Cross-cutting:* the fastest loop is `←/→` step + a **hold-to-rock/flip key** (the step every amateur tool misses) + a verdict key that auto-advances. Procreate Dreams' warmth lives in haptics, gesture, earned metaphor, and responsiveness — *zero* skeuomorphic decoration — which is the whole "tactile without twee" proof.

### B · AI generation studios

*Studied: Google Flow (hardest — "not a Flow clone"), Krea, Higgsfield, Runway, LTX Studio, ComfyUI, Freepik.*

- **Flow's "stack": every edit is a non-destructive version; four *named* regen modes** (Regenerate / Edit prompt / Reference / Extend). *Steal:* anima's retry ladder made visible — a vertical cel-stack per frame, each carrying prompt + verdict; the red critic mark on rejected cels (top-15 #10). *Avoid:* Flow's frictionless four-button row — anima's regen isn't free, so each carries its cost + gate state. — Flow help.
- **Runway Aleph's verb-led delta grammar:** "describe the change; everything unmentioned is preserved." *Steal:* the edit box invites "change the stylus to the left hand," not a re-description (matches Bea's `ONLY CHANGE:` discipline). *Avoid:* the "no masks, no keyframes, magic" framing that hides the human decision. — Runway Aleph guide.
- **Flow "Ingredients": named, typed, reusable reference chips** (character / object / style). LTX auto-extracts "Elements" + persistent profiles; Runway coaches "re-attach the same reference, use the same name." *Steal:* promote the chip to a **folder-chip** (a character = its Bible); surface a cast rail + Bible-health inline + the actual per-frame recipe (top-15 #9). *Avoid:* Flow's opacity about which ingredient was used. — Veo Ingredients; LTX; Runway Gen-4 References.
- **ComfyUI's honest failure: "most people don't want to look at a node graph."** Every fix is progressive disclosure (App Mode, Nodes-Map tree, Subgraphs = "a folder"). *Steal:* the principle, not the graph — anima's stepper is the linear spine; the DAG is an on-demand "why did this re-run" inspect view (top-15 #8). *Avoid:* both the raw graph *and* the "App Mode" over-correction that hides all wiring. — ComfyUI subgraphs/app-mode writeups.
- **Powerful vs toylike:** tools feel *powerful* with a legible lever of predictable effect (Flow's camera copy-values bar); *toylike* when they bury you in undifferentiated presets/models (Higgsfield's effects wall, Krea's 64 models) or hide levers behind "magic." *Steal:* keep every anima lever named and predictable; steal Flow's copy-values-across-clips for holds/placement. *Avoid:* an "effects wall" and "magic, trust us." — Chase Jarvis review; goenhance review.

*Cross-cutting:* pipeline tools show "where am I" with a *linear layer over the graph* (ComfyUI Nodes-Map; Flow's 3-mode spine; LTX's left-to-right progression) — anima already has the strongest version (the resumable stepper). Flow feels like a template because it leads with the box, hides cost/critique, and personifies nothing — anima inverts all three.

### C · Dailies / review / approval tools (most directly relevant)

*Studied: Frame.io (hardest), Autodesk Flow Production Tracking / ShotGrid, ftrack, SyncSketch, Filestage.*

- **Annotation is welded to a note** (Frame.io: draw + write, `Enter` submits together; the mark *is* the instruction). *Steal:* every red-pen stroke or pin carries a text note = the edit instruction forwarded to NB2; hide marks during playback, summon on click. *Avoid:* a hard note-first ordering — keep the note optional, the *welding* mandatory. — Frame.io commenting.
- **Two spatial grains:** pin (one click → point-comment at exact coords) vs red pen (freehand region). *Steal:* pin = "the leg count is wrong *here*," pen = "redraw this hand"; arm each with a single key + an armed-state cursor change. — Frame.io anchored comments.
- **The annotation data model = normalized 0–1 coordinate objects, not a flattened raster.** *Steal (load-bearing for D1):* persist pin/pen/box as normalized vector objects + the welded note; to forward to NB2, either rasterize onto the frame at native output resolution (exact at any size) or translate the mark's location into a spatial clause. This is the answer to the spec's open "annotation → NB2 form" question. — Frame.io developer docs.
- **The fast loop: JKL + single-key everything + auto-pause-on-type + approve-and-advance.** Decision key advances to the next asset via `[`/`]`; typing in the comment box auto-pauses; `?` summons the cheat sheet. *Steal:* wholesale (top-15 #2); add a `?` cheat-sheet overlay (accessibility + onboarding backstop for a keyboard-first gate). *Avoid:* in/out range-marking (anima reviews single frames — reclaim those keys). — Frame.io shortcuts; SyncSketch.
- **Compare: wipe slider + pixel-difference + linked zoom.** *Steal:* wipe to A/B two retries; pixel-diff to catch identity drift against the anchor; linked zoom to inspect a hand/eye on both at 200% (top-15 #4). *Avoid:* the format-agnostic machinery (anima only compares still-vs-still). — Frame.io Comparison Viewer.
- **Onion-skin / ghosting + "jump to next annotated/unreviewed frame."** *Steal:* ghost the anchor under the candidate (top-15 #3); traverse to the next frame *needing attention*, skipping approved ones. — SyncSketch.
- **Status as a color edge, three states, framed as iterate-forward** ("Needs work," not "Rejected"). *Steal:* a teal-bright edge = approved on the reel, neutral = unreviewed, pulse = retrying — the loop's review state legible at a glance. *Avoid:* per-reviewer status matrices, notification broadcasts, @mentions, client-review sessions — every bit is team infrastructure a solo creator drops. — Frame.io/Workfront; Filestage; ftrack.

*Cross-cutting:* the fastest loop is `look → Enter (approve + advance) → look → …` for clean frames, and `look → R → type (auto-pauses) → pin/pen → Enter (re-roll + return)` for a bad one. Spatial annotation feeds the edit via normalized coordinates → rasterize-as-control-image and/or spatial-clause-in-prompt. Team bloat to drop: approval matrices, notifications, threads, client sessions, in/out ranges, brush studios.

### D · Pro-tool craft aesthetics

*Studied: Linear, Figma, Raycast, Arc, Cursor, Superhuman, Notion.*

- **Luminance hierarchy: dim the chrome, spotlight the work** (Linear's refresh: "don't compete for attention you haven't earned"). *Steal:* anima's warm chrome sits visibly lower-contrast than the dark stage — the stage is where the eye goes. — Linear refresh.
- **Depth from a tonal surface ladder + hairlines, never shadows/glows** (Raycast: a 3–4-step dark ladder, tight 16–24px padding). *Steal:* define a dark ladder for stage panels (frame well / strip / verdict rail), separate by tonal step + hairline. — Raycast DESIGN.md.
- **Speed *is* the anti-sterile move** (Superhuman's 100ms rule, 32–60ms in practice; Arc's warning that pure minimalism is "sterile"). *Steal:* hold the eye-gate + ⌘K under 100ms perceived; let the *one* soulful element be Sean's pencil character + warm paper. — Superhuman; Arc/Browser Company.
- **⌘K to five rules:** available-everywhere + open/close on the same key, central, omnipotent, forgiving fuzzy + aliases, intent-ranked, and it **teaches its own accelerator** (right-aligned single-key hint). *Steal:* wholesale (top-15 #7); aliases in Sean's vocabulary ("ship"/"lock"/"approve" all hit approve). — Superhuman command-palette post.
- **Shortcut *grammar*, not a lookup table** (Linear: single letters act on the object, `G`-prefix = "go to," `?` = cheat sheet). *Steal:* bare keys for eye-gate verdicts, a `G`-prefix to jump stages/shots, `?` for the overlay. — Linear shortcuts.
- **Taste micro-details against a flat system:** Raycast's lone keycap gradient; one OpenType feature as a fingerprint; Notion's hover-revealed handles; empty states as onboarding; opinionated microcopy ("Mark Done," not "Archive"). *Steal:* JetBrains-Mono keycap chips, one signature Newsreader detail (tabular figures for counters), hover-revealed per-frame controls, monochrome pencil-register empty states, an opinionated action vocabulary. — Raycast; Notion; Superhuman.
- **Delight that isn't a gimmick:** Raycast's confetti earns its place by being *useful* (a test signal) and executed to the full quality bar; the anti-example is a cheap Google easter egg. *Steal:* one quiet, earned delight — the *ship moment* when a full loop passes, in the pencil register, executed to the bar. — Unsung/Raycast.

*Cross-cutting:* non-gimmicky delight is either a byproduct of speed/intelligence (Cursor's jump, Superhuman's flow) or one deliberate flourish held to the product's bar. Clean+dense+fast without sterile = luminance hierarchy + tonal-ladder depth + soft/subtracted separators + tight padding + hover-hidden affordances + sub-100ms speed + one scarce accent + warm-not-cold neutral (Linear literally shifted its gray warmer — validating `#FBF5E9`). A great palette = fuzzy + aliases + intent-ranking + teaches-its-shortcut, not a filtered `<select>`.

### E · Gallery / lightbox / museum presentation

- **White cube subtracts the world; black box is the correct neutral for a lit/moving subject.** A pencil-test *loop* is exactly the luminous subject the black box was invented for. *Steal:* the *subtraction* doctrine and the dark wall; the honesty that dark is theatrical, not neutral (that "under evaluation" read is the point). *Avoid:* pure black (crushes the pencil's low tones) — `#15161A` is the correct near-black warm-neutral. — O'Doherty via Artforum; Déméter (Lille).
- **Light-pool, not flood; intensity sets the mode of looking.** A narrowed beam on the work (dark surround) is the visual hierarchy; high-contrast single pool = "reverence/judge," soft even wash = "relaxed stroll." *Steal:* a soft light-pool/vignette-inverse around the judged frame; use contrast level to distinguish "judging one frame" from "touring the finished run." *Avoid:* a skeuomorphic spotlight-cone graphic. — Bannolighting; Alcon.
- **The mat = engineered negative space; oversized mat = "museum-quality."** *Steal:* a generous isolating mat band around every judged frame; judging controls + wall-text live *outside* it; save the lavish oversized mat for the Museum's hero shots (impression), not the working stage (inspection). — Logan Graphic; framing trade.
- **57" center-line: variable-size frames centered on one horizon read as a composed wall, not a grid.** *Steal:* the judged frame's optical center on a fixed screen eye-line; center a sequence on one horizon. *Avoid:* top-aligning variable-height frames (reintroduces the thumbnail-grid feeling). — Ocular-ergonomics.
- **Two-tier wall text beside, never on, the work:** factual tombstone + a 50–100-word, ≤25-words-per-sentence interpretive layer; one consistent template across the exhibit. *Steal:* on the stage — a terse tombstone (shot/act/frame/cast/criteria); in the Museum — Mo's docent "why we committed to pro." *Avoid:* long walls of text, caption-over-artwork, bespoke per-exhibit layouts. — V&A guidelines.
- **Lightbox = dim/blur the page to recede everything but one image** (the digital white-cube + spotlight fused). Standard affordances: Esc/arrows, obvious exits, full-viewport on mobile, deep-zoom for inspection (OpenSeadragon). *Steal:* the dim IS anima's "warm → dark judge" transition; deep-zoom pan/zoom for line-quality + identity inspection; comparative overlay for draft-vs-pro. *Avoid:* trapping the user, centered floating boxes on mobile. — NN/g overlays; OpenSeadragon.
- **Simultaneous contrast** — the science under the stage: dark surround makes the frame pop (great) but lifts perceived brightness (the palette caveat → the gray color-check toggle). — UMN perception text.
- **Curated linear scrollytelling + before/after sliders** (Google Arts & Culture's Story Editor; Nasjonalmuseet; JuxtaposeJS). *Steal:* pin the frame, scroll the decision-story; sliders as "proof of transformation"; curate to decision-moments. *Avoid:* a free-browse grid as the primary Museum experience; scroll-jacking. — GA&C; Nasjonalmuseet.

*Cross-cutting:* a single work reads "under judgment" via subtraction + a light-pool + a mat + a constant center-line horizon (all four, dark-surround-with-the-honest-color-caveat). The best online walkthrough is a curated linear narrative from a repeatable template, sticky-pin scrollytelling, before/after sliders — and its edge over a physical wall is that it shows the *process*, which is exactly anima's differentiator. Wall text is a subordinate two-tier label beside the work, one consistent template.

### F · Timeline / NLE patterns (the minimal subset)

*Studied: DaVinci Resolve (Cut page), Final Cut Pro, After Effects, Premiere, iMovie/CapCut.*

- **DaVinci's Cut page is the charter:** a whole second, simpler editor — "drop footage → arrange → trim → deliver, everything live, one click," no tracks/tools/bins. *Steal:* this *is* anima's timeline — and "finish in a real editor" = anima being the Cut page and handing off to the Edit page. *Avoid:* every Edit-page element by name (track stacks, tool-mode toolbar, bins, color/audio/compositing). — Blackmagic Cut page.
- **Arrange = long-press-to-lift → drag → drop, neighbors auto-close the gap** (iMovie). *Steal:* the lift gesture + an always-contiguous, gap-free ordered array (reorder = index swap). — iMovie.
- **Trim = hover an edge → double-arrow cursor + handle → drag in/out, hard-stopping at true source, a ghost of available source, `←/→` frame-nudge.** *Steal:* exactly this — the whole trim scope. *Avoid:* slip/slide/roll, ripple-with-consequences, tool auto-selection. — Shotcut; iMovie; DaVinci.
- **Magnetic *outcome*, not machinery.** FCP's magnetic timeline exists to make gaps/collisions impossible — but for one lane you get that free from an ordered array + local **edge-snapping** (a hairline snap-line on abut). Freeform (Premiere) is actively wrong (it taxes you with manual gap-closing + the silent 1-frame black gap). *Steal:* snapping as permanent + unlabeled; never freeform. — FCP; No Film School.
- **Playhead vs skimmer; loop-by-default.** Hover-skim any card to peek that frame in the player without moving the playhead; **loop the whole sequence continuously** as the primary review mode (the one deliberate divergence from every NLE, which buries loop). *Steal:* both (top-15 #14). — FCP; Premiere/CapCut.
- **One poster-frame card, width = duration, single teal selection border; two-tier export.** *Steal:* the cleanest clip representation + a primary GIF/WebM/MP4 + resolution export, anything heavier buried (iMovie Share). *Avoid:* dense in-card filmstrips, codec/bitrate panels. — FCP; iMovie.

*Cross-cutting:* the minimal timeline is the Cut page in one lane — magnetic behavior (ordered array + snapping), hover-reveal trim handles, hover-skim + loop-by-default, poster-frame cards width=duration, two-tier export. Magnetic outcome yes, magnetic *system* no, freeform no. Everything else an NLE offers is out of scope by anima's own charter.

### G · "Personal" / presence / companion software

- **Personalization = your fingerprints, transparently.** Granola renders *your* notes in black, the AI's in gray (authorship stays visible). Copilot/ChatGPT show "memory updated," viewable/editable/deletable. UX Tools: "people treasure what they tweak… the apps we remember are the ones that remember us back." *Steal:* a legible, *editable* "what the studio remembers about your taste" panel (registers, ratified verdicts, "you reject digital-render looks," "your loops run ~7 heads tall"); Sean's own configuration is the unfakeable, non-creepy personalization. *Avoid:* a silent model that "just knows." — Granola; Copilot Memory; UX Tools.
- **Agent identity = role + consistent voice, not avatars or backstory.** In a crew, names are wayfinding + trust-routing: you need to know whose judgment you're reading. *Steal:* anima's Maya/Sam/Bea/Cy/Flo/Em/Mo are correct-by-construction — persona = phase + stable working register; introduce each by role. *Avoid:* backstories, moods, "excited to help!!" — that turns a crew Sean *directs* into a cast performing *at* him. — dev.to multi-agent framework.
- **Restraint is the anti-cringe control.** Emoji off, no exclamation, locked traits; over-anthropomorphism *reduced trust ~47%* in one cited study (single-source — directional). *Steal:* the Newsreader/JetBrains-Mono wire-service register IS this. Em says "Palette drift on frame 3: hair reads warm-brown, spec is ash. Proposing recolor clause." — not "Ooh, I noticed something! 😊." — Pixelmojo.
- **Never over-claim past capability (the Clippy law).** Clippy *looked* alive but couldn't think; the one craft win was "treat the box as a stage — let it enter and exit." *Steal:* the dark judging stage is anima's literal bounded stage; Em is honest about not seeing motion; agents propose, don't decide. *Avoid:* an agent volunteering unprompted or implying more understanding than the model has. — Notion/Sinofsky.
- **Sense of place + environmental storytelling.** Arc: "designed for me, not the average user"; the 2005-Mac "software had a soul"; BioShock's objects tell you who lives here. *Steal:* let the studio *fill with Sean's artifacts* — bibles pinned up, the reject pile with failure codes, the growing museum, roughs on the easel — evidence a real animator works here; and "unnecessary" craft that says someone cared (comparison GIFs, the hand-drawn red mark, paper grain). *Avoid:* an empty onboarding-fresh studio that never accrues his stuff (reads as a demo). — Arc; Ryo Lu; environmental-storytelling.
- **Ritual: proactive-on-open, interruptible-always; a logbook, not a streak.** Poke lives where you already are and can be interrupted like a human; Duolingo's streak is the cautionary dark-pattern (guilt serves the metric, and anima has no engagement metric to serve). *Steal:* "what's on your easel today" waiting on open (the shot to judge, Em's overnight note, the draft ready to escalate) that Sean redirects; a quiet studio logbook ("3 loops shipped this week," the museum grown), progress *reflected* never *demanded*. *Avoid:* nagging notifications, sad-face pressure, loss-aversion streaks. — Poke/TechCrunch; Duolingo dark-patterns.
- **Humor at the seams only.** Warmth injected at non-critical seams (loading, empty states, waits) says "relax, humans work here" (Discord loading screens); never during judgment or on Sean's actual frames. *Steal:* one dry line in Sean's comedic voice on the render-wait / empty easel. *Avoid:* forced whimsy everywhere. — UX Tools.

*Cross-cutting:* "mine, with my crew" = configuration you can see your fingerprints on + memory that reflects you back transparently + a studio that accrues your artifacts. Presence without cringe = honesty × restraint × role (never feelings about Sean or itself). The tasteful morning ritual = a studio logbook + easel that greets him proactively-on-open but interruptible-always, warmth at the seam, work at the center.
