REEL ONE
the screening room.

Dailies culture, made an interface. The frame is the only lit object in the room; chrome is warm-black with film grain, recedes when idle, and returns when you reach for it. Notes are timecode-addressed. Judging animation means running the projector — and the decision verb is the one animation directors have always used: print it. A century of screening-room ritual is the interaction model.

Mood — Higgsfield renders, soul_2
How it serves the soul

Engine truth is the arbiter — this direction is built around it. You cannot judge animation from a still, so the room's sacred act is running the loop: hold Space and the projector rolls at 12 fps with a breath of gate weave; release and it freezes on your frame. Lights-out is one key. The medium is judged in the medium.

The human owns the decision — as dailies ritual. Em calls notes from the back row, timecode-stamped, lamp-lit (PRINT / HOLD), and never touches the projector. Approving circles the take on the camera report — the director's mark since the paper era. The cascade isn't a spinner; it's an Academy leader counting down to the next picture.

Iteration is cheap — "go again" is one key and a prefilled note, because re-shooting a take is the cheapest thing a studio does. The box-office stub keeps spend visible without ever dimming the picture.
