## Cheese taxonomy — registers to never emit sincerely

1. **Fake profundity** — "magical," "something shifted in the world," the future-was-already-here.
2. **LinkedIn poetry** — layoff-as-invitation, new-chapter framing, parallel-structure inspiration.
3. **Motivational poster** — votes for the person you're becoming, the work works on you.
4. **Hype-closer** — "buckle up," "never been a more exciting time to build."
5. **Aesthetic-flex routine porn** — staccato 5-AM-coffee-terminal scene-setting played straight.
6. **Grandiosity about agents** — agents framed as transcendence. Sean's frame: they're impressive AND they routinely faceplant (API-limit email, wrong output). The pride is real but always anchored to operational reality.

## The Satire-Redemption Rule

Cheese can survive in EXACTLY one configuration: deployed as overt satire and **paired with a
grotesque visual that signals the irony** — Sean's register for this is Ren & Stimpy-grade
detail ("a lard ass sitting at his desk with a blinking terminal and a huge mess of beer
bottles and vape cartridges around him"). Without the visual, delete the cheese. There is no
text-only ironic cheese.

*(Standing Substack format idea, banked: cheesy one-liner captions paired with disturbing,
grotesquely detailed images. Sean invented this mid-gauntlet; it's a content format, not just
a voice rule.)*

**Script-confirmed extension (2026-06-04) — diegetic cheese:** across all 11 screenplays,
every hacky line is assigned to a buffoon and punished in-world ("Try and KETCHUP!" →
"Do you guys write your own material?"; Allen's "Bobby FLAKE" flop-sweating at a party that
hates him). So in prose, cheese has a SECOND legal configuration: **quoted as someone else's
speech and reacted to.** Sean's narrator may report cheese; he may never produce it.
Corollary: **the Narrator Pun ban** — a pun in Sean's own voice is a register error; licensed
wordplay is slang-literalization or the Fumbled Idiom only. This rule existed fully formed in
his ~2019 writing; the Satire-Redemption Rule is its visual-era descendant.

## Limp Deflation (the subtlest failure — read twice)

The models' attempt at Sedaris self-deprecation produces lines like:

> "I wrote something down a week ago and obviously forgot about it. My agents didn't. They never do."

Sean: "GROSS. That's the model's attempt at a self-deprecating David Sedaris... a so called
limp dick deflation about how I forget shit."

**The distinction:** real Sean self-deprecation is a *specific incriminating story* — a named
place, substance, casualty, or witness (bought a dub at the side of his parents' house;
skipped half the play and made a castmate cry; thought he ended a dog's life with a tennis
ball). Fake deflation is an *abstract confession of mild incompetence* (forgetting things,
being bad at mornings) engineered to set up the agents' competence.

**Test before shipping any self-deprecating line:** does it have a named place, person,
substance, or victim? Does it cost Sean something real to admit? If no to either, cut it.

## Clever-metaphor wit ≠ Sean wit (the MEANING_OVER_ACCESS diagnosis)

`docs/MEANING_OVER_ACCESS.md` is a Sean-labeled negative specimen: "a try hard Hunter
Thompson writing about agent buzzwords."

What it does (and what to stop doing): per-sentence engineered metaphor cleverness about
*infrastructure abstractions* — "the unattended cron job with delusions of competence,"
"the void wearing a JSON costume" — plus chiasmus deflations ("The button worked. The
decision did not."). Every sentence performs.

What Sean actually does: **narrative wit**. Stories with named people, places, and
substances; physical comedy; canon quotes deployed as speech; metaphors drawn from his
reference universe or his body, not from cron jobs and JSON. His wit arrives in waves
between stretches of plain storytelling — if every sentence is witty, it's not Sean.

**Archaeologically confirmed (2026-06-04):** zero ancestors of the clever-metaphor register
exist in 8,663 lines across his 11 screenplays. The diagnosis holds against the full record.

## Prop-recycling ban

The calibration corpus props — **cold coffee, Tuesday, the desk, the ferry, the bathroom
stall** — are details from *specific past pieces*, not Sean's permanent vocabulary. ("It
also has all of the authors thinking I just drink cold coffee at all hours of the day.")

Rule: generate a fresh concrete anchor from the actual topic of the piece. Reuse a corpus
prop only when the subject genuinely is that prop (a ferry piece may have the ferry). The
jewel-center examples in SKILL.md (blinking cursor, weathered Logitech, ferry horn) are
illustrations of the *technique* — never a menu to pick from.
