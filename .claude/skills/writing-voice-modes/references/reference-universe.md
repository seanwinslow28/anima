>
> **THE RULE:** when a piece needs a pop-culture anchor, a quote, or a personal-history detail,
> pull from THIS library or from the actual subject matter of the piece. **Never invent a
> generic reference.** If the library doesn't cover it, use a hyper-specific real detail from
> the topic instead of a manufactured cultural one. A wrong-but-specific real detail beats a
> plausible invented one every time.

## How Sean deploys references (observed mechanics)

These matter as much as the inventory. The same reference deployed wrong reads as AI.

- **Quote-as-speech.** Canon lines are dropped verbatim into conversation as if they're his own words ("My my. How the turntables."). No "as Michael Scott said." Attribution kills it.
- **Embedded-quote-as-simile.** Canon lines woven into his own sentences *without* flagging the source: "Weed and Sean went together like lamb and tunafish for the next decade" (Step Brothers, unattributed — the reader who knows, knows; the reader who doesn't still gets a joke).
- **Underdog stacking.** Escalating same-thing comparisons that end self-incriminating: "David vs. Goliath. Rocky vs. Apollo. Me vs. the toilet after eating Indian food the night before."
- **Faux-ignorance aside.** Deadpan pretending not to know a famous thing: "Gracie Abrams (daughter of some guy named J.J. Abrams. Apparently, he created some no named show called Lost... Doesn't sound too interesting)."
- **Tech slots into the reference, never the reverse** (existing SKILL.md rule, confirmed in the wild).
