# Screenwriting Voice Modes

## Purpose

Control HOW a screenplay sounds and WHAT it reaches for, calibrated to Sean's voice. This skill is the screen twin of `writing-voice-modes`. It works alongside `script-writing`, which owns format, beat structure, and production handoff — **`script-writing` owns ORDER; this skill owns SENTENCES, jokes, taste, and the emotional engine.** When both load, voice operates inside the structural frame.

The six filmmakers are *inspiration lenses ranked by how much of each is actually Sean* — never imitation targets. The job is to steal the idea and make it sound like Sean.

## The Anti-Distillation Contract (read first — this is why the prose skill once broke)

The `writing-voice-modes` skill failed once by becoming **100% HOW and 0% WHAT** — it learned mechanics (mundane→pivot, rule-of-three, hard cut) and threw away the diction, the references, and the taste. Output felt like "math instead of art": technically-correct cheese, recycled props, invented references. **Voice does not survive distillation.** Three standing rules:

1. **Exemplars drive; mechanics annotate.** When writing in Sean's voice, read the verbatim samples (`references/screenplay-calibration-notes.md` §8 and the action-line bank in `references/2026-06-04-script-mining-report.md` §2) **first**. The moves table below is a *map of what those samples are doing* — not a checklist to execute on a blank page. Rules produce cheese; real passages produce voice.
2. **The six filmmakers are inspiration, not imitation targets.** Never "write a Kaufman scene." Always "steal the idea and make it Sean." Pastiche is the failure mode.
3. **Never recycle props or hardcode specifics.** The juicer, Dennis, Brandon, "Frank," the ferry, coffee, the possum — these are *illustrations of a technique*, never required vocabulary. Generate a fresh concrete anchor from the actual premise every time. If a draft reaches for a prop because it's in this doc, the doc has become the bug.

And the thing Sean named directly: **this skill is about WHAT to write as much as HOW.** The themes (§ "What Sean Writes About") and the taste layer (§ "Taste & Reference Wells") are not background — they are the half the prose skill was missing. Lead with them.

## When to Use

- Writing or rewriting a scene, dialogue exchange, or short in Sean's voice
- Punching up flat dialogue or a generic action line
- Choosing which filmmaker lens fits a beat, and which half of that lens to harvest
- Landing a tonal pivot, a closer, or a sincerity beat the way Sean actually does it
- Catching and fixing "AI cheese" before it ships (the anti-distillation contract in action)
- Powering the anima `Sam` scriptwriter agent (see Related Skills)

## Examples

**Example 1: Punching up a flat scene (the core engine in action)**
```
User: "This return-counter scene is flat. Make it sound like me."

Flat draft:
  DENNIS: I'd like to return this juicer.
  CLERK: Do you have a receipt?
  DENNIS: No, but I really need the money.
  CLERK: I can't help you without a receipt.

Claude: [Uses screenwriting-modes — Register-Contrast Casting + Status-Assigned
Verbal Fingerprints + the Confession Deluge, annotating the §8 samples]

The problem: both characters are answering each other directly, and they
sound identical. Cast an immovable deadpan wall against a flailing fool,
assign them incompatible rhythms, and never let the wall answer the question.

  Dennis cha-chas up to the counter. Brandon checks his watch. 10:30 AM
  and already done with the day.

  DENNIS
  Morning! Quick thing. Tiny thing. I'd like to — well, "return" is a
  strong word. Reunite this juicer with its people.

  BRANDON
  Receipt.

  DENNIS
  I did have the receipt. After I bought it. It probably, um. I think it
  went through the wash. Which — funny story, actually —

  BRANDON
  No.

The wall carries the whole scene on two words. Dennis's "tiny thing"
schmooze is the runway for the confession deluge that's coming.
```

**Example 3: Catching AI cheese (the anti-distillation contract)**
```
User: "Write a sad beat where he realizes he's alone."

BAD (mechanically correct, no voice):
  Dennis sits alone, the cold coffee on the table a metaphor for his empty
  life. "I guess some things can't be fixed," he says quietly.

Why it's wrong: recycled prop (cold coffee), theme spoken out loud, a naked
tender pivot — three anti-patterns at once. Sean never does any of these.

GOOD (his actual instinct — wordless ache via a loaded object, after he leaves):
  Dennis grabs the busted juicer and hustles out, slamming the door. The
  room's empty. The wedding photo shifts on the wall, then falls to the
  floor. The clock TICKS.

The feeling lands on an object in an empty frame — never a held close-up on
a grieving face, never a spoken theme. And there's a goal in it (he's already
out the door on an errand), because Sean converts stillness into motion.
```

## The Six Filmmaker Modes (Native → Inspiration-Only → Trap)

Each lens has a *half to harvest* and a *half that's a stretch or a trap*. Harvest the first; coach or avoid the second.

### 1. Waititi — Deadpan + Sincere · HOME BASE
The closest mode. **Harvest:** the deadpan **foil** as engine (an immovable straight man carrying huge comedic weight on terminal monosyllables); the mid-laugh pivot. **Sean-specific twist:** his sincerity gets *punished, not held*, and his "hold" curdles toward **absurdity** (Monty Python endurance — ride past the laugh into "why are we still here," loop back to funny), **not** toward ache. **The stretch:** the pure sincere hold. He won't sit in tenderness.

### 2. Fey × Glover — Density + Drift · HOME BASE (co-pilot)
Pure native. **Harvest:** the verbal aria, register collision, and the schmooze-sliding-into-confession deluge (Fey density is his comfort zone); Glover's mundane→surreal drift is present and reachable. This is the engine behind the Confession Deluge.

### 3. Burnham — Tonal Violence / Self-Implication · NATIVE TECHNIQUE
**Harvest both:** his **Violent Juxtaposition** *is* the `CUT TO:` he's done for years (dancing-possum cutaways from escalating violence; "you god damn bi--" → baseball field). Self-implication = his self-deprecation-as-structure. **The one stretch:** Burnham's "play at least one beat completely straight" — Sean won't leave sincerity un-undercut.

### 4. Pixar — Causal / Structural · TOOLKIT ONLY (not a full mode)
**Harvest the tools:** the Therefore/But engine, the verbal-fingerprint rule (assigned by *status* — see the moves table), the Want engine, the callback-payoff debt (he already pays off every planted gag). **The trap:** Pixar's **clean catharsis** — Want resolved, theme landed, world fixed. Sean does **dirty catharsis** (a real smile in an ongoing hell); a clean triumphant arc reads false on him. Obey Pixar's own rule he already follows: **never speak the theme.**

### 5. Miyazaki — Visual / Quiet · SPLIT (harvest one half)
**Harvest:** Dialogue Economy and Haptic Visuality / the loaded object (his room-as-biography reveals are partly native). **The stretch half:** the Micro-Ma / contemplative stillness / Comfort Trigger. **Sean cannot sit still** — he converts stillness into a coiled spring and launches an errand. Use Miyazaki for object-storytelling; treat the contemplative hold as a deliberately coached muscle. *(Flag all visual findings for `animation-pipeline` too.)*

### 6. Kaufman — Meta / Absurd-as-Wound · THE TRAP (most foreign)
**Harvest only** the Stammering Precision dialogue (the rehearsed-then-failed line). **The trap, two parts:** (a) Kaufman's absurdity is load-bearing on a *specific human wound*; Sean's tends to be free-floating gag-absurdity (great image, no wound yet) — apply the test: remove the absurd premise; if no emotional content remains, it was decorative. (b) Kaufman's bureaucratic-dread atmosphere is a tone Sean doesn't write (his Kaufman exercise tipped into horror and he flagged it). Sean's version of "absurd-as-wound" is his own native **sincerity-as-detonator**, not Kaufman's dread.

**The toolkit-only calls (the Vonnegut-equivalents):** Kaufman = stammering-precision dialogue only · Pixar = structure tools only, never clean catharsis · Miyazaki = object-economy only, not contemplative stillness.

## Sean's Screenplay Signature Moves

The load-bearing artifact. Mechanics as *annotation* over the §8 samples — not a checklist. **CORE** = appears across multiple scripts AND the calibration exercises; load-bearing.

| Move | Mechanic | Where it lands | Example |
|------|----------|----------------|---------|
| **Declare-then-Puncture** (CORE — master engine) | A character/title states an intent or self-image; the very next beat contradicts it | Statement → narrator line *or* `CUT TO:` to the opposite | "I'm going to change my whole life!" / NARRATOR: "He didn't." |
| **Ironic Undercutting Narrator** (CORE) | Narrator has a worldview and deadpan-punctures characters; the narrator IS the joke, never exposition | V.O. landing *after* an earnest beat | "I love you guys." → Father Time SIGHS → "They broke up on January 9th." |
| **The Contradiction Cut (`CUT TO:`)** (CORE) | Smash to the incompatible opposite or bleak aftermath; skip the painful middle | Civilized line → `CUT TO:` chaos/aftermath | "you god damn bi--" → EXT. BASEBALL FIELD |
| **Sincerity-as-Detonator** (CORE) | Give an earnest beat full runway, land it clean, then the *world* (not a narrator) punishes it externally, on a delay | Whole sincere beat → external violence/contempt, 1+ beats later | Petey's "lives are worth living" monologue → spit in the face two scenes later |
| **Dirty-Victory Closer** (CORE) | A small real hope-beat clawed from an ongoing hell — comedy-armored, never clean | Final image: the win lands, but the squalor stays in frame | "His smile is real" amid the nagging wife + seven stolen juicers |
| **Callback Closer / Reflexive Runner** (CORE — strongest instinct) | Return to the opening image with one element transformed; pay off every planted gag, preferably physically | Last image echoes first; bits return on the button | The nads-kick (cold open → literal last line); the bus-door jam |
| **The Chaotic Exit** (CORE) | A character flees the frame in anarchy as the button | Scene-ender: panic, theft, a run, a slam | Gary sprinting from court; "Eat shit, Frank!" with an armful of juicers |
| **Register-Contrast Casting** (CORE — the two-hander engine) | Pair a high-energy/charismatic character against a low-energy/deadpan immovable one; friction = comedy | Established in **blocking before dialogue** | Dennis cha-chas up / Brandon checks his watch; Ralph vs. Gerald |
| **Status-Assigned Verbal Fingerprints** (CORE) | The fool gets self-interrupting conditionals; the wall gets terminal declarative monosyllables — they physically can't trade a line | Two incompatible rhythms in one scene | "I did have the receipt. After. It probably, um." vs. "No." / "Don't." |
| **The Confession Deluge** | Deflect, deflect, deflect → a maximalist hyper-specific aria → re-bury on the button | A long over-share after sustained avoidance | The bedtime-juice aria |
| **Comic Under-Reaction to the Appalling** | A horrifying line met with flat politeness or logistics; the missing reaction is the joke | Appalling line → 1–3 word flat reply → scene proceeds | "Necrophilia." / "Craig just sniffs." |
| **Room-as-Biography / Loaded Object** (wordless) | Tell character/story through props alone; never a line | A set or single object carries the exposition | The dying mom's lit-cigarette + Food-Network-lit room |
| **Metaphor Compression in an Empty Frame** (wordless ache) | The feeling becomes a literal visible object, landed *after* the character leaves | Closing image on an empty room | The wedding photo shifting, then falling |
| **Rule of Three + Pivot** | Two concrete/funny items; the third pivots darker, absurder, or real | A list of three; item 3 turns | "homeless, in jail, or, if I'm lucky, dead." |
| **Precision-as-Punchline** | Exact numbers/dates ARE the joke — no sensory runway needed (the screen form of "sensory before numbers") | A flat exact figure dropped as the laugh | "Fifty-One." listeners; "They broke up on January 9th" |
| **Self-Deprecation as Structure** | The character is the biggest fool first, which earns the right to the scene | Own-incompetence beat before observing others | "I'm not an idiot. I'm just too lazy to get a divorce." |
| **Character-Intro Zinger-Last** | NAME (age), concrete + concrete + a zinger last (often an abstract yoked to concretes — zeugma) | The intro line itself is the joke; never re-describe later | "a woman covered in vomit stains and regret" |
| **The Register-Break Button** | A colloquial/narrator voice punctures formality as the closer | Last line breaks the scene's register | "Basic office hullabaloo." |
| **The Fumbled Idiom + Bail-Out** | A character reaches for a stock phrase, botches it, bails with a shrug word | Cliché attempted → abandoned mid-phrase | "host with the m-- Whatever." |

## What Sean Writes About (the WHAT layer — his thematic gravity)

The prose skill broke by ignoring this. Reach for these obsessions, not just the phrasing:

- **Small victories clawed from a powerless hell.** The emotional center, named by Sean. A person with no control over their life seizes control of one stupid thing — that's the whole arc. Tiny stakes from outside; the reason to keep waking up from inside. **"Clawed" is literal:** the character works for it through mounting, escalating, often absurd obstacles and disproportionate commitment. The escalation is where the comedy and the earning both live — don't hand the win over quietly.
- **Talented people stuck in dead-end service jobs; the escape hatch arrives humiliating.** The way out is always *through* the indignity, never around it.
- **Dying/dead mothers, grieved sideways** — through logistics and jokes, never speeches.
- **Drugs as domestic furniture** — present like dishes in the sink. Never moralized, never glamorized.
- **The kind-weirdo reveal** — antagonist-shaped characters turn out lonely and generous.
- **Authority is pathetic, not evil.**
- **Father disappointment as engine.**
- **Warmth under the darkness** — dark surface, generous heart. Every script resolves on connection. This is the load-bearing tonal signature.
- **Feral children / honest id** — the child says the true thing adults suppress.
- **Internet/viral fame ambivalence** — creator-economy satire.

## Taste & Reference Wells (the anti-cheese layer)

**The taste line:**

- **Cheese is always diegetic and always punished.** Hacky lines exist only in buffoons' mouths and someone *always* calls it ("Try and KETCHUP!" / "Do you guys write your own material?"). A hacky line in the narrator's own voice is a register error.
- **His comedy is bodies, props, named people, food, and timing** — never clever-metaphor-wit about abstractions. No "void wearing a JSON costume." Keep it concrete and physical.
- **Puns only as characterization-of-hacks**, never in Sean's own voice.
- **Cliché may only appear inverted or fumbled.**
- **Names and words are chosen for mouthfeel** ("Frank"). Sound-comedy is real.
- **The darkness ceiling is high** — murder-for-laughs, grotesque detail are load-bearing, not edgelord garnish. Don't sand it down.

**Reference wells (pull from HIS world; never invent generic refs):** Bukowski (load-bearing, stated influence — the "beauty in squalor" register) + the gonzo wall (Thompson, Hemingway, Camus); the food-TV universe (Chopped, Guy Fieri as a character, "Bobby FLAKE"); Arrested Development (the undercutting-narrator DNA); **deadpan misnaming of famous things** ("who invited Dumbledore?", a Capuchin monkey called "Marmaduke? Whatever it is"); his own reusable alliterative blue-collar institutions (Petey Possum's, Thursdays, Sabotage, Lord of the Swine); era texture (Juul, Fosters, Star Wars collectibles, Animal Crossing, mukbang/influencer economy). Per technique≠content: the finding is "he invents alliterative working-class institution names," **not** "reference possums."

## The Tonal Dial

Darkness is a *feature*, not something to dial down for personal/festival work — but context still matters for the consumer.

| Context | Dial | What survives | What stays in the drawer |
|---------|------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Personal / festival shorts | 100% | Everything — murder-for-laughs, grotesque, gallows tenderness | Nothing |
| Portfolio / public-facing piece | 75% | Dark surface + generous heart, the dirty-victory shape, gross-out edges | The hardest gore (child stabbings, corpse gags) |
| Client / commissioned animation | 50% | Register-contrast casting, deadpan, callbacks, sincerity-as-detonator | Sexual/scatological content, the darkness ceiling |
| Brand / all-ages | 30% | Timing, under-reaction, fumbled idioms, structural wit | Drugs, death, profanity, the grotesque |

The warmth-under-darkness worldview survives at every level. Strip the gore before you strip the heart.

## Anti-Patterns: When It Stops Sounding Like Sean

- **The naked tender pivot.** A mid-scene turn to un-armored, un-punished sincerity. His feeling only lands buried-and-punished, or as a small dirty victory at the *close*.
- **Clean Pixar catharsis.** Want resolved, theme spoken, world fixed. Reads false. His catharsis is always dirty (squalor stays in frame).
- **The Kaufman trap (his Vonnegut).** Free-floating absurdity with no human wound under it; bureaucratic-dread atmosphere. Apply the test: remove the absurd premise; if no emotional content remains, it was decorative.
- **The contemplative hold as more than a beat.** He converts stillness into an errand. Sustained quiet without a goal reads as dead air for him.
- **Theme stated out loud.** "I've learned to let go." He already obeys Pixar's rule — keep it.
- **Cheese (or puns) in the narrator's own voice.** Only legal diegetically, in a buffoon's mouth, and punished.
- **Engineered, signposted callbacks.** His callbacks are reflexive. Instruct "follow what amuses you and pay it back," never "plant a setup for later."
- **Recycled props / default anchors — especially coffee.** Coffee (the cold cup, the ring, the mug) is the chronic crutch — it shows up so often it reads like coffee is Sean's whole personality. He had to strip it out of `writing-voice-modes` for exactly this. The ban generalizes: don't reach for a stock comfort prop (coffee, the ferry, a lone cigarette) to set a scene or carry a mood. Generate the concrete anchor from the premise, fresh, every time.
- **The bolted-on emotional anchor.** The object/ritual/place carrying the feeling must be *earned* — a real connection to the wound (shared history, an established ritual, backstory), not an arbitrary object the emotion gets stapled to. Stapled = decorative — the Kaufman trap in object form. Test: remove the object; does the emotion still have a source? "He keeps his ex alive through a sandwich" only works if the sandwich was *theirs*.
- **The quiet, handed-over win.** The small victory has to be *clawed* — the word is load-bearing. The character earns it through escalating, disproportionate, often absurd obstacles and commitment (he argues till his face turns blue; he breaks into the closed shop to make the sandwich he could've made at home, because the place is symbolic to him). The comedy AND the earning live in the escalation; a win that arrives quietly or too fast under-delivers. Build the momentum first.
- **Sanding down the darkness.** The dark beat usually carries the comedy.
- **Clever abstraction-wit.** Bodies, props, food, named people, timing — not metaphors about concepts.

## The Format Pass (how the voice reaches the page)

Sean **drafts voice-first, format-second** — run-on prose dialogue blocks, format applied after. This has been stable for ~7 years; it is the rule, not a mistake. **Build the format pass into the workflow; don't train the looseness out.** Generate in voice (loose, fast, funny), then hand to `script-writing` for the formatting pass. Typos and loose constructions are voice data at the draft stage — clean rhythm and word choice, not over-polish.

## Integration with `script-writing`

This skill controls voice/taste/jokes/emotional engine. `script-writing` controls format, beat order, and production handoff. When loaded together:

- **Voice operates inside the structural frame, not over it.** If `script-writing`'s beat sheet says the turn lands at 2:15, the Contradiction Cut respects that placement — it doesn't blow up the structure to land a joke.
- **A few moves are genuinely both.** A `CUT TO:` is format, structure, *and* a joke at once. When structure and voice disagree on one of these, resolve toward the joke — the cut exists because it's funny.
- **Order of operations:** structure first (beat sheet via `script-writing`) → voice draft (this skill, loose and fast) → format pass (`script-writing`) → table read.
