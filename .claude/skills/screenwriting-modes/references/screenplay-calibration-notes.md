# Screenplay Voice Calibration Notes — Sean Winslow

> Distilled from a 10-question interview, 5 writing exercises, and a full read of Sean's 11 screenplays (2026-06-15).
> This is the synthesis, not the transcript. Three sources are braided here: the **exercises** (live diff-based calibration on a shared premise — a man returns a used juicer with no receipt), the **six filmmakers** the skill draws on (Kaufman, Waititi, Miyazaki, Pixar, Burnham, Fey × Glover) treated as *inspiration ranked by nativeness*, and Sean's **former scripts** (the 11-script mining report + direct reads of the five sketches). Use alongside SKILL.md when calibrating screenplay output.

---

## 0. The Anti-Distillation Contract (read first — this is why the prose skill broke)

The `writing-voice-modes` skill failed once by becoming **100% HOW and 0% WHAT** — it learned the mechanics (mundane→pivot, rule-of-three, hard cut) and threw away the diction, the references, and the *taste*. The result felt like "math instead of art": technically-correct cheese, recycled props ("cold coffee on a Tuesday"), invented motivational-poster references. **Voice does not survive distillation.** Do not repeat that here.

Three standing rules for any screenplay output:

1. **Exemplars drive; mechanics annotate.** When writing in Sean's voice, look at the verbatim samples in §8 (and his actual scripts) *first*. The moves table in §6 is a map of what those samples are *doing* — it is not a checklist to execute on a blank page. Rules produce mechanically-correct cheese; real passages produce voice.
2. **The six filmmakers are inspiration, not imitation targets.** The job is never "write a Kaufman scene." It is "steal the *idea* and make it sound like Sean." The authors are scaffolding and shared vocabulary; Sean is the target. Pastiche is the failure mode.
3. **Never recycle props or hardcode specifics.** The juicer, Dennis, Brandon, "Frank," the ferry, coffee — these are *illustrations of a technique*, never required vocabulary. Generate a fresh concrete anchor from the actual premise every time. If a draft reaches for a prop because it's in this doc, the doc has become the bug.

And the whole point Sean named: **this skill is about WHAT to write as much as HOW.** §4 (themes) and §5 (taste/references) are not background — they are the half the prose skill was missing. Lead with them.

## 1. Interview Findings

### Q1 — Scene entry gear
**A + C.** The Rugrats macro-pull (open tight on a grotesque/specific detail, rack out to the scene — the establishing shot *inverted*) fused with **in medias res**. Not "Fade in: once upon a time" — "PLOP, you're already behind, catch up." The V.O. narrator is *not* his way in; it's a separate instrument he bolts on.

### Q2 — The ironic narrator: tool or crutch
**The narrator is the joke**, never exposition. Specifically a **contradiction engine** — character declares, narrator deadpan-punctures: "I'm going to change my whole life!" / NARRATOR: "He didn't." (Arrested Development DNA.) Both *I Quit* and *New Year's* are **trope autopsies** — the narrator stands outside a cliché (the quit-your-job-for-family movie, the New-Year's-reinvention myth) and flicks it. Protect this device.

### Q3 — On-the-nose vs. oblique
Said **default oblique**; directness is a *marked* move he uses as a **genre signal** (the on-the-nose "I'm tired of lying to our son, Gary" is parody costume, mocking melodrama). **The page revised this:** obliqueness isn't blanket — it's **assigned by role**. The fool evades; the immovable straight-man states bluntly. So the real answer is closer to Q3's option C ("depends on who's the fool") than the A he gave.

### Q4 — Verbal fingerprints / what makes two people incompatible
Said **worldview / philosophical difference**. **The page revised this, then Sean corrected my over-read:** he is **engine-agnostic** — sometimes he starts from "these two are philosophically opposed," sometimes he "lets it ride and sees what comes out," and he likes owning both. The reliable invariant under all of it is **casting contrast**, not the engine (see §3).

### Q5 — Is the gut punch even wanted
Proven body of work leans **deflation / sketch / bits**. *But the scripts corrected my first read:* comedy-with-feelings is already native — just not as Pixar catharsis. His ache arrives as **sincerity-as-detonator** (full earnest runway, then the *world* punishes it) and as the **small dirty victory** at the close. The genuinely aspirational thing is *un-punished, clean* catharsis. (See §3.)

### Q6 — The tonal pivot, in screenplay form
**A + B**, and they turned out to be the same instinct as Q2. The `CUT TO:` is "I promise" → cut → he does the exact opposite — the *visual* twin of the narrator's "He didn't." His Deadpan Hold is **not** Waititi sincerity; it's the **Monty Python endurance hold** that rides past the laugh into "why are we still here" and loops back to funny. His hold curdles toward *absurdity*, not ache.

### Q7 — How endings resolve
**C — both the laugh and the ache.** Relate-then-laugh-at. The prose **callback closer** transfers completely to the page (proven unprompted in Exercises 1 and 4). His closers are his single strongest, most reliable instinct in *both* media.

### Q8 — Words vs. pictures
**B — verbal-first, actively building the wordless muscle.** Petey Possum's paddle-ball-to-the-face open shows the muscle already training. *The exercises refined it:* the wordless muscle is real but it's a **visual-narrative** muscle (object reveals, room-as-biography), **not** a contemplative-ache muscle. Flag everything here for `animation-pipeline` too.

### Q9 — Ensemble vs. two-hander
**Pairs.** The two-hander is his generative unit, and his pairs are a grounded one and a chaotic one with the feeling living in the gap (the *Drug Deal* straight-man-addict vs. goofy-heartfelt-dealer shape). Crowds (New Year's) are the exception he reaches for when the *premise* is "a room all failing the same way."

### Q10 — The capstone
**Home base: Fey × Glover density-and-drift + Waititi deadpan-sincere.** **Most foreign: Kaufman** — but precisely: not the dialogue or awkwardness (he loves and can use those), and not "he can't do absurd" (he does constantly). The trap is that Kaufman's absurdity must be **load-bearing on a specific human wound**, and Sean's absurdity tends to be **free-floating gag-absurdity** (great image, no wound yet); plus Kaufman's **bureaucratic-dread atmosphere** is a tone Sean doesn't write. This is the Vonnegut-equivalent finding.

## 2. Mode Ranking (Native → Inspiration-Only → Trap)

The six filmmakers are inspiration lenses. Here is how much of each is actually Sean, and which *half* of each to harvest.

1. **Waititi (deadpan + sincere) — HOME BASE.** Closest mode (he left the Waititi draft in Ex1 untouched). *Sean-specific twist:* his deadpan **foil** is the engine (the immovable straight man carrying huge comedic weight on terminal monosyllables), but his sincerity gets **punished, not held**, and his hold curdles to **absurdity** (Python) rather than ache. Harvest: the deadpan-foil casting, the mid-laugh pivot. Mind: the pure sincere-hold is the stretch.

2. **Fey × Glover (density + drift) — HOME BASE (co-pilot).** The verbal aria, the register collision, the schmooze-sliding-into-confession deluge are pure native (see the juice aria, §8). Fey density is his comfort; Glover's mundane-to-surreal drift is present and reachable.

3. **Burnham (tonal violence / self-implication) — NATIVE TECHNIQUE.** His **Violent Juxtaposition** *is* the `CUT TO:` he's done for years (the dancing-possum cut-aways from escalating violence in *Petey Possum*; "you god damn bi--" → baseball field in *I Quit*). Self-implication = his self-deprecation-as-structure. Harvest both. The one Burnham move that's a *stretch*: "play at least one beat completely straight" — Sean won't leave sincerity un-undercut.

4. **Pixar (causal/structural) — TOOLKIT ONLY, not a full mode.** Harvest the **Therefore/But engine**, the **verbal-fingerprint** rule (assigned by status — see §6), the **Want engine**, and the **callback payoff debt** (he already pays off every planted gag). *The trap:* Pixar's **clean catharsis** — the Want resolved, the theme landed, the world fixed. Sean does **dirty catharsis** (a real smile in an ongoing hell); a clean triumphant arc reads false on him. Also heed Pixar's own rule he already obeys: never speak the theme.

5. **Miyazaki (visual/quiet) — SPLIT. Harvest one half.** His **Dialogue Economy** and **Haptic Visuality / the loaded object** are reachable and partly native (his room-as-biography reveals, §6). *The stretch half:* the **Micro-Ma / contemplative stillness / Comfort Trigger.** Sean cannot sit still — he converts stillness into a coiled spring and launches an errand (Ex5). Use Miyazaki for object-storytelling; treat the contemplative hold as a deliberately-coached muscle.

6. **Kaufman (meta / absurd-as-wound) — THE TRAP (most foreign).** Harvest *only* the **Stammering Precision** dialogue (the rehearsed-then-failed line — he can use it). *The trap, two parts:* (a) Kaufman's absurdity is load-bearing on a **specific human wound**; Sean's is free-floating gag-absurdity — apply the research's own test: remove the absurd premise; if no emotional content remains, it was decorative. (b) Kaufman's **bureaucratic-dread atmosphere** is a tone Sean does not write (his Ex1 Kaufman draft tipped into horror and he flagged it). Sean's version of "absurd-as-wound" is his own native **sincerity-as-detonator**, not Kaufman's dread.

**The toolkit-only calls (the Vonnegut-equivalents):** Kaufman = stammering-precision dialogue only. Pixar = structure tools only, never clean catharsis. Miyazaki = object-economy only, not contemplative stillness.

## 3. Key Calibration Discoveries

### Declare-then-puncture is his core engine — three devices, one move
The ironic narrator (Q2), the `CUT TO:` (Q6), and the prose hard-cut are **the same instinct in three media.** "I'm going to change my whole life!" / "He didn't." is identical in shape to "I promise" → CUT TO → he does the opposite. Everything else radiates from this. *Evidence:* it recurs across every script and every exercise.

### His tonal pivot vector is DOWN/chaotic, never UP/tender — mid-scene
Handed an explicit "funny → real" turn (Ex3), Sean **refused the sincere pivot.** His "real" was the *con collapsing* (the weaponized fake-family bid + "a pathetic fake smile that makes him look constipated"), and both directions he picked — the hard `CUT TO:` to bleak aftermath, and the loaded-silence detonation ("Eat shit, Frank!") — go down or into chaos. He explicitly rejected the pure hold (Burnham's "play it straight" is his stretch).

### …but the CLOSER can go up — as a small *dirty* victory (this is his real emotional center)
Ex4 corrected the above with a crucial placement rule: **sincerity lives at the close, never the mid-scene turn, and it's always a small victory clawed from an ongoing hell.** Dennis sips his juice, "His smile is real" — but it's armored by the nagging cuckolding wife, seven stolen juicers, the basement of Star Wars collectibles. Not Pixar's clean win — a real smile inside a life that's still garbage. Sean named the thesis himself: *"it's about the small victories."*

### Sincerity-as-detonator is his native comedy-with-feelings (not deflation, not catharsis)
The scripts prove the ache was always there, in a specific shape: give an earnest beat **full runway**, land it **completely**, then let the **world** (not a narrator) punish it on a delay. Petey's "I was born to remind people their lives are worth living" → the doctor cries → two scenes later the birthday boy spits in his face. Lliam's "I love you guys" → Father Time SIGHS → "They broke up on January 9th." The juice aria → Brandon: "You didn't buy this here, did you?" **Naked, un-armored, un-punished mid-scene sincerity is the only genuine stretch.**

### The reliable invariant is register-contrast CASTING, not the conflict engine
Sean is engine-agnostic (worldview-first or discovery-first by feel), but the *casting* never varies: a **high-energy/charismatic** character friction-fit against a **low-energy/deadpan immovable** one. Dennis (cha-cha-ing, schmoozing) vs. Brandon (checks watch, "No." "Don't."); Ralph vs. Gerald; Father Time vs. the room. *What* they fight about is improvised; *the energy split* is the engine. The deadpan foil carries enormous comedic weight on almost no lines.

### Verbal fingerprints are assigned by status/role
The fool gets **self-interrupting conditionals** ("I did have the receipt. After I bought it. It probably, um. I think–"); the wall gets **terminal declarative monosyllables** ("Hello." "Mhmm." "No." "Don't."). They physically cannot trade a line. He establishes the split in **blocking before dialogue** (Dennis cha-chas up; Brandon checks his watch — 10:30 AM and already done).

### His obliqueness is a flood, not a spike — deflect → over-confess → re-bury
Not Pete-and-Tim sustained indirection. Sean's shape is: deflect, deflect, deflect → a **maximalist hyper-specific confession deluge** (the aria) → re-bury on the button ("let's not get into semantics"). His directness arrives as a *flood*, and the flood is where both the comedy and the feeling live.

### Callbacks are reflexive, not engineered — and they thread across scenes
"Frank" paid off because he *enjoyed* it ("sounds like your mouth is farting"), not because he architected a runner. Across the exercises he unconsciously threaded Alejandro (Ex2 aria → Ex4 wife), the Star Wars collectibles, the duffle bags, the mushroom. **Never instruct him to "plant a callback for later."** Instruct: *follow the thing you find funny and return to it.* That's how his form.

### Wordless = a visual-narrative muscle (objects/rooms), not a contemplative one
Ex5: asked to *sit in* a loss, Sean converted it to **plot and motion** — discovery (the basement betrayal told entirely through props) then an **errand** (check the clock, store's open till 7, grab the juicer, leave). **His escape from stillness is a goal, not a line of dialogue** — he even installed a ticking clock and a deadline that weren't asked for. His door into wordless *feeling* is the **symbolic object in an empty frame** (the wedding photo shifting and falling *after* he leaves) — never a held close-up on a grieving face. Critical for the animation pipeline: he'll board gorgeous storytelling-through-stuff; the contemplative emotional hold must be coached.

### The darkness always carries a comic burr
Even the betrayal reveal (Ex5) has the "large obnoxious purple dildo exposed" in the duffle. He does not do clean pathos; every sad beat has a grotesque joke in it. The corpus ceiling is high (murder for laughs, child stabbings, a mother dead under a fart) — higher than his prose register allows.

## 4. What Sean Writes About (the WHAT layer — his thematic gravity)

The prose skill broke by ignoring this. These are the obsessions that recur across all 11 scripts — *what to reach for*, not just how to phrase it.

- **Small victories clawed from a powerless hell.** The emotional center, named by Sean. A person with no control over their life seizes control of one stupid thing, and that's the whole arc. The stakes look tiny from the outside; to the character they're the reason to keep waking up.
- **Talented people stuck in dead-end service jobs; the escape hatch arrives humiliating.** The way out is always *through* the indignity, never around it (Brandon's viral-humiliation cooking show, Eric's paid cuckolding that yields a literary agent). This is Sean's own biography's shape, written years before it happened.
- **Dying/dead mothers, grieved sideways.** Grief processed through logistics and jokes, never speeches; mothers numbed by wine/weed and television.
- **Drugs as domestic furniture.** Coke at a wake, weed oil for a cancer patient, AA as a sales channel. Never moralized, never glamorized — present like dishes in the sink.
- **The kind-weirdo reveal.** Antagonist-shaped characters turn out lonely and generous (Ralph the dealer; Wulfgang the cuckold-millionaire-turned-friend).
- **Authority is pathetic, not evil.** The menu-throwing manager is fighting rats; the cops are just fit.
- **Father disappointment as engine.** Jack's entire possum quest is "Who's the loser now, Dad?!"
- **Warmth under the darkness.** The register is dark; the worldview is generous. Every script resolves on connection. This is the load-bearing tonal signature — **dark surface, generous heart.**
- **Feral children / honest id.** Children (and child-like characters) say the true thing adults suppress.
- **Internet/viral fame ambivalence** — creator-economy satire he was writing before working in tech.

## 5. Taste & Reference Wells (the anti-cheese layer)

### The taste line
- **Cheese is always diegetic and always punished.** Hacky lines exist only in buffoons' mouths and someone *always* calls it ("Try and KETCHUP!" / "Do you guys write your own material?"; Lliam's "Self-deprecation. Love it." gets the group-stare). A hacky line in the narrator's *own* voice is a register error.
- **His comedy is bodies, props, named people, food, and timing** — never clever-metaphor-wit about abstractions. There is no "void wearing a JSON costume" anywhere in 8,663 lines. Keep it concrete and physical.
- **Puns only as characterization-of-hacks**, never in Sean's own voice.
- **Cliché may only appear inverted or fumbled** — his characters can't even complete a stock phrase ("host with the m-- Whatever").
- **Names and words are chosen for mouthfeel** ("Frank"). Sound-comedy is real to him.
- **The darkness ceiling is high** — don't sand it down. Murder-for-laughs and grotesque detail are load-bearing, not edgelord garnish.

### Reference wells (pull from HIS world, never invent generic refs)
- **Bukowski** — load-bearing, stated influence ("The Laughing Heart" recited nearly in full in *NBAGT*); plus the gonzo wall (Thompson, Hemingway, Camus). *Missing from the author stack — the "beauty in squalor" register is all over his scripts.*
- **The food-TV universe** — Chopped, Food Network, Guy Fieri (as a character), Bobby Flay ("Bobby FLAKE"), Duff Goldman. Two whole scripts live here.
- **Arrested Development** — the undercutting-narrator DNA.
- **Reusable alliterative blue-collar institutions** — he builds his own fictional IP and ports it across scripts: *Petey Possum's, Thursdays, Sabotage, Lord of the Swine, Exotic Aquatic.* The finding is "he invents alliterative working-class institution names," not "reference possums."
- **Deadpan misnaming of famous things** — "who invited Dumbledore?", "Marmaduke? Whatever it is" (a Capuchin monkey), the Ace-of-Cakes guy. Under-naming the famous thing is pervasive and funny.
- **Lexicon items (2+ scripts):** "lose their shit," "shit-eating grin," "Son of a bitch." as a complete resigned sentence, "lofty dreams," junk food measured "by the sleeve."
- **Era texture:** Juul, Fosters, Star Wars collectibles, Animal Crossing, Gameboy, mukbang/influencer economy.

## 7. What Doesn't Work for Sean (traps & anti-patterns)

- **The naked tender pivot.** A mid-scene turn to un-armored, un-punished sincerity. He deflects it every time (Ex3). His feeling only lands buried-and-punished, or as a small dirty victory at the *close*.
- **Clean Pixar catharsis.** Want resolved, theme spoken, world fixed. Reads false on him. His catharsis is always dirty (squalor stays in frame).
- **The Kaufman trap (his Vonnegut).** Free-floating absurdity with no human wound under it; bureaucratic-dread atmosphere. He generates great absurd images but tends to lack the wound — apply the test: remove the absurd premise; if no emotional content remains, it was decorative.
- **The contemplative hold / Miyazaki Ma as more than a beat.** He cannot sit in stillness; he converts it to an errand. Sustained quiet without a goal reads as dead air for him, not contemplation.
- **Theme stated out loud.** "I've learned to let go." He already obeys Pixar's rule — keep it.
- **Cheese in the narrator's own voice.** Cheese is only legal diegetically, in a buffoon's mouth, and punished. Same for puns.
- **Engineered, signposted callbacks.** Telling him to "plant a setup for later" — his callbacks are reflexive; instruct "follow what amuses you and pay it back."
- **Recycled props / hardcoded specifics** (the prose skill's literal bug). Generate fresh anchors from the premise.
- **Sanding down the darkness.** His ceiling is high; the dark beat usually carries the comedy.
- **Clever abstraction-wit.** Bodies, props, food, named people, timing — not metaphors about concepts.
- **Format-naïve drafting treated as a mistake.** He drafts voice-first, format-second (run-on prose dialogue blocks), and has for ~7 years. Build the format pass into the workflow; don't train the looseness out.

## 8. Voice Samples (verbatim — the primary instrument)

These are the calibration anchors. When writing in Sean's screenplay voice, read these *before* the moves table.

### Sample 1 — The Confession Deluge / Sincerity-as-Detonator (Ex2)
Dennis, after deflecting the clerk through schmooze, a wacky-product bit, and a fake-name runner, finally floods:

> "I spent 10 hours cooped up in [a] fluorescently lit cubicle that could make Rikers Island feel like a Caribbean spa. I've spent a decade typing up TPS reports and occasionally going on drug runs for an undeserving nepo baby who got the managerial role I should have because his Daddy thought giving him more responsibility would straighten out his coke habit. I have carpal tunnel in my right hand and blue balls from a wife that's always too tired to have sex after she comes home from her 1 on 1 training sessions with a very handsome Puerto Rican man with a ponytail on his head and a hog in his pants. I'm not an idiot. I'm just too lazy to get a divorce. […] There's just one thing that I get to look forward to each night. […] That one thing is my bedtime juice."
> Brandon stares at Dennis, unperturbed.
> BRANDON: "You didn't buy this here, did you?"

*Why it's gold:* rule-of-three-at-macro-scale pivoting to "juice" (laugh AND ache in one object), self-deprecation as structure, the litany of hyper-specifics, and the comic under-reaction that punishes the sincerity. This is Fey-density + Waititi-pivot made entirely of Sean.

### Sample 2 — The Dirty-Victory Closer (Ex4)
> Dennis presses the start button, WINCES, and shields his face. It WORKS. […] Thick gold sludge fills the glass. He drinks. Closes his eyes. Pure bliss. Behind him, seven more juicers line the counter, all stickered.
> [Beth enters in workout gear, a hickey on her neck, nagging about the juicers and the basement and "Alejandro's right—"]
> Beth's voice trails off. Dennis' eyes remain shut as he sips through her shrill nagging. His smile is real.
> **Today, we spell victory: D-E-N-N-I-S.**

*Why it's gold:* the small victory clawed from a powerless hell, armored on every side by comedy and squalor; the register-break narrator button. Not clean catharsis — *dirty* catharsis.

### Sample 3 — The Callback Closer (Ex4, the transformed return)
> Dennis awkwardly tosses the bag over his shoulder. Juicers bashing against his back. […] He walks on the bus, but jerks back. He turns around. The bag is stuck in the door. […] Dennis' smile fades and his eyes fill with fear. He aggressively tugs and pulls, making an absurd amount of noise.

*Why it's gold:* returns to Exercise 1's opening image (the juicer jammed in the door, the "absurd amount of noise") with one element transformed — he *won* this time, and the door defeats him identically. The world doesn't care that he won.

### Sample 4 — Wordless ache via the loaded object (Ex5)
> Dennis swiftly makes his way into the kitchen […] grabs the busted juicer and hustles out of the front door, slamming it behind him. The room's empty. The wedding photo shifts, then falls to the floor. The clock TICKS.

*Why it's gold:* the only pure-ache beat he allowed, and he could only land it through a *symbolic object in an empty frame*, after the character left. His door into wordless feeling — never a held face.

### Sample 5 — Canonical narrator detonator (from *New Year's Resolutions*)
> LLIAM: "And mine… is to strengthen my relationships. And host this party every year. I love you guys."
> Everyone smiles and cheers. Father Time just SIGHS.
> FATHER TIME: "[the deadpan statistical demolition]"

*Why it's gold:* the undercutting narrator + sincerity-as-detonator in his own published voice — the reference standard the exercises were reaching toward.

## 9. Phase 4 Note — Skill Architecture Recommendation

**Recommendation: mirror the prose architecture and split into two skills.** Rationale, from Sean's own `storytelling-architecture` load-bearing rule — *"structure owns ORDER, voice owns SENTENCES; if the structure skill writes sentences, its phrasing flattens voice"* — a risk that is *worse* in screenplay, where beat sheets generate generic connective dialogue and action.

- **`script-writing`** = format + structure + production handoff (the screen-equivalent of `creative-writing` + `storytelling-architecture`, fused because on the page beat-order and format are one artifact).
- **`screenwriting-modes`** (new, twin of `writing-voice-modes`) = the six filmmaker modes as inspiration + this document's signature-moves table + the WHAT layer + the voice samples. This is the calibration layer, isolated so structural phrasing can't flatten it.

So: prose = `creative-writing` + `storytelling-architecture` + `writing-voice-modes`; screen = `script-writing` + `screenwriting-modes`.

**Two caveats:** (1) screenplay fuses voice/format/structure harder than prose (a `CUT TO:` is format, structure, *and* a joke at once), so a few signature moves must be cross-referenced from both skills; (2) this is Sean's call and it does not block anything — these notes are valid input either way. Lock the architecture at Phase 4 build time.

**Carry the WHAT layer (§4–§5) and the exemplars (§8) into whichever skill houses the voice — they are the half the prose skill was missing, and the reason it once felt like math instead of art.**
